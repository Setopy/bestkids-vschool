Ω Languages · Week 8

French Immersion III: Daily Life

The big questionIn French, Tuesday is mardi and Friday is vendredi. In English they are named after Tyr and Frigg. Both languages are naming the same two planets. What do the days of the week remember?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Name the days of the week and the parts of the day in French.
  2. Ask and tell the time.
  3. Conjugate regular -er verbs and the verb être in the present tense.
  4. Describe a daily routine and make a sentence negative.

1Les jours de la semaine

The Romans named the days after the Sun, the Moon and five planets, which carried the names of their gods. French kept the Roman names. English replaced four of them with the matching Norse gods from Week 4: Mars became Tyr, Mercury became Woden, Jupiter became Thor, and Venus became Frigg.

French does not use capital letters for days or months. The week begins on lundi.

4Regular -er verbs

Most French verbs end in -er and follow one pattern. Remove the -er to find the stem, then add the ending.

The endings -e, -es and -ent are silent, so four of the six forms sound exactly alike. That is why French must always state the subject pronoun, while Spanish can leave it out.

Before a vowel or silent h, je shortens to j': j'aime, j'habite, j'écoute.

5Saying no

To make a sentence negative, wrap the verb in ne … pas.

Je parle français. I speak French. Je ne parle pas français. I do not speak French.

Before a vowel, ne becomes n': Je n'aime pas le lundi. I do not like Mondays.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Conjugate jouer for "we" and "they", and translate: "Le samedi, nous jouons au football. Mes frères ne jouent pas."

  1. Stem: jou-. With nous add -ons: nous jouons. With ils add -ent: ils jouent.
  2. Le samedi means "on Saturdays".
  3. ne jouent pas is the negative.

Answer: nous jouons, ils jouent. "On Saturdays we play football. My brothers do not play."

Example 2

Say in French: "It is half past six in the morning. I eat and I listen to the radio."

  1. Half past six: six heures et demie. In the morning: du matin.
  2. I eat: je mange. I listen: j'écoute, with j' before the vowel.

Answer: Il est six heures et demie du matin. Je mange et j'écoute la radio.

Hands-on: Ma Journée

You need: paper, coloured pens, a clock with movable hands or a drawn clock face

  1. Draw six clock faces showing six moments of your day.
  2. Under each write the time in French and one sentence about what you do: À sept heures, je mange. À neuf heures, j'étudie.
  3. Make a weekly timetable with the seven days in French across the top. Fill in one activity for each day.
  4. Write three things you like and three you do not like, using J'aime … and Je n'aime pas …
  5. Present your day aloud. A parent moves the clock hands and asks Quelle heure est-il ? five times.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Which planet is vendredi named after, and which English day matches it?

    Answer and why

    Venus; Friday

    WhyFrench kept the Roman name. English substituted Frigg, the Norse goddess matched with Venus.

  2. Say "It is quarter past three" in French.

    Answer and why

    Il est trois heures et quart.

    Whyet quart means "and a quarter". Time is always given with Il est … heures.

  3. Conjugate regarder with tu and with vous.

    Answer and why

    tu regardes, vous regardez

    WhyAdd -es for tu and -ez for vous to the stem regard-.

  4. Translate: "Elle est française et elle habite au Mexique."

    Answer and why

    "She is French and she lives in Mexico."

    Whyest is the "she is" form of être, and habite is the "she" form of habiter.

  5. Make this sentence negative: "Nous travaillons le dimanche."

    Answer and why

    Nous ne travaillons pas le dimanche.

    Whyne goes before the verb and pas after it: "We do not work on Sundays."

  6. Why must French always state the subject pronoun?

    Answer and why

    Because several verb forms sound identical.

    Whyparle, parles and parlent are all pronounced "parl", so only the pronoun shows who is speaking.

Write six sentences in French describing a perfect Saturday. Include two times, at least four different -er verbs, one sentence with être, and one negative sentence.

Answer and why

Answers will vary. Example: "Le samedi, je ne travaille pas. À neuf heures, je mange avec ma famille. Je suis content."

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • The days of the week preserve gods that hardly anyone has worshipped for over a thousand years. Why do such names survive?
  • French verbs sound alike but are spelled differently. Would it be better to spell them as they sound?
  • Which is easier for you so far, French grammar or French pronunciation? What does Spanish help with, and where does it mislead you?
