∑ Mathematics · Week 8

Variables

The big questionThink of any number. Double it. Add 6. Halve the result. Subtract the number you first thought of. Your answer is 3. It is always 3, for every number in existence. How can anyone be sure without testing them all?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Use a letter to stand for an unknown or changing number.
  2. Write and evaluate algebraic expressions.
  3. Simplify expressions by collecting like terms.
  4. Solve one-step and two-step equations by keeping the balance.

1A name for the unknown

In the bar models of Weeks 4 and 5 you drew a box and called it "1 unit". A variable is the same idea with a shorter name: a letter that stands for a number we do not yet know, or one that can change.

WordMeaningIn 3n + 5 = 17
VariableA letter standing for a numbern
CoefficientThe number multiplying a variable3
ConstantA number on its own5 and 17
TermA part separated by + or −3n, 5, 17
ExpressionTerms with no equals sign3n + 5
EquationA statement that two expressions are equal3n + 5 = 17

In algebra we do not write the × sign. 3n means 3 × n, and ab means a × b. Division is written as a fraction: n ÷ 4 is written n/4.

To evaluate an expression, replace the letter with its value and follow the order of operations from Week 3. This is called substitution.

3Like terms

Terms with exactly the same letter part are like terms, and they can be added or subtracted, just as 4 apples and 2 apples make 6 apples.

4a + 2a = 6a. But 4a + 2b cannot be combined, and neither can 4a + 3, because apples are not bananas or plain numbers.

4Equations are balances

An equation is a pair of scales in balance. Whatever you do to one side you must do to the other, or the balance tips. To find the variable, undo each operation with its inverse, working in the reverse of the order of operations.

−+

÷×

Always check by substituting your answer into the original equation.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Evaluate 3n + 5 when n = 4, and simplify 4a + 3 + 2a − 1.

  1. Substitute: 3 × 4 + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17.
  2. Collect the a terms: 4a + 2a = 6a.
  3. Collect the constants: 3 − 1 = 2.

Answer: 3n + 5 = 17 when n = 4; and 4a + 3 + 2a − 1 = 6a + 2

Example 2

Solve 3n + 4 = 25.

  1. Undo the + 4 by subtracting 4 from both sides: 3n = 21.
  2. Undo the × 3 by dividing both sides by 3: n = 7.
  3. Check: 3 × 7 + 4 = 25. ✓

Answer: n = 7

Example 3

Sara has 5 more than twice as many shells as Tom. Sara has 31 shells. How many does Tom have?

  1. Let t be Tom's number of shells. Sara has 2t + 5.
  2. Equation: 2t + 5 = 31.
  3. Subtract 5 from both sides: 2t = 26. Divide both sides by 2: t = 13.
  4. Check: 2 × 13 + 5 = 31. ✓

Answer: Tom has 13 shells.

Hands-on: The Mystery Bags

You need: a ruler balanced on a pencil or a kitchen balance, small identical coins or beans, three paper bags

  1. Secretly put the same number of beans in each of two bags. Place the two bags and 3 loose beans on one side, and 11 loose beans on the other. It balances.
  2. Your partner writes the equation (2b + 3 = 11) and solves it by removing the same thing from both sides.
  3. Open the bags to check. Swap roles and make harder puzzles, with bags on both sides.
  4. Now perform the "think of a number" trick from the top of this lesson on three people.
  5. Write the trick in algebra, using n for the secret number. What happens to n?

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Write an expression for "4 less than six times a number".

    Answer and why

    6n − 4

    Why"Six times a number" is 6n, and "4 less than" that means subtract 4 from it.

  2. Evaluate 2(x + 3) − 4 when x = 5.

    Answer and why

    12

    WhyBrackets first: 5 + 3 = 8. Then 2 × 8 = 16, and 16 − 4 = 12.

  3. Simplify 7b + 4 − 3b + 6.

    Answer and why

    4b + 10

    WhyThe like terms 7b and −3b make 4b. The constants 4 and 6 make 10.

  4. Solve x + 9 = 23.

    Answer and why

    x = 14

    WhySubtract 9 from both sides. Check: 14 + 9 = 23.

  5. Solve 5y = 45.

    Answer and why

    y = 9

    WhyDivide both sides by 5. Check: 5 × 9 = 45.

  6. Solve n/4 − 2 = 3.

    Answer and why

    n = 20

    WhyAdd 2 to both sides to get n/4 = 5, then multiply both sides by 4. Check: 20 ÷ 4 − 2 = 3.

  7. A rectangle is 4 cm longer than it is wide. Its perimeter is 36 cm. Find its width.

    Answer and why

    7 cm

    WhyLet the width be w, so the length is w + 4. The perimeter is 2(w + w + 4) = 4w + 8. Then 4w + 8 = 36 gives 4w = 28 and w = 7. Check: 2 × (7 + 11) = 36.

★ Scholar's challenge

Use algebra to prove that the trick in the opening question always gives 3. Then invent a trick of your own that always gives 5.

Answer and why

Starting with n: doubling gives 2n; adding 6 gives 2n + 6; halving gives n + 3; subtracting n leaves 3.

WhyThe variable n stands for every possible number at once, so one line of algebra proves the result for all of them. For a trick that always gives 5, add 10 in place of 6: (2n + 10) ÷ 2 − n = 5.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Testing a million numbers cannot prove the trick always works. One line of algebra can. What is the difference between evidence and proof?
  • Is x in an equation "unknown" or "variable"? Can it be both?
  • The word algebra comes from the Arabic al-jabr, "restoring", in the title of a book by al-Khwarizmi written about AD 820. What is being restored when you solve an equation?
