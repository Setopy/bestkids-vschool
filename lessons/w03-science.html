Atomic Structure & the Bohr Model

The big questionIf an atom were the size of a football stadium, its nucleus would be a small marble on the centre spot, and almost everything else would be empty. If atoms are nearly all empty space, why can you not walk through a wall?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Name the three subatomic particles with their charges and locations.
  2. Use atomic number and mass number to count protons, neutrons and electrons.
  3. Draw Bohr diagrams for the first twenty elements.
  4. Describe how the model of the atom changed as new evidence appeared.

1Inside the atom

An atom is the smallest piece of an element that is still that element. It has a tiny, dense centre called the nucleus, surrounded by electrons.

ParticleChargeWhereRelative mass
Proton+1In the nucleus1
Neutron0In the nucleus1
Electron−1Around the nucleusabout 1/1,836

A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so the charges cancel. Nearly all the mass is in the nucleus.

You cannot walk through a wall because the electrons of your atoms repel the electrons of the wall's atoms. Like charges push apart.

2Two numbers that describe an atom

  • The atomic number is the number of protons. It decides which element the atom is. Six protons is always carbon. Seven is always nitrogen.
  • The mass number is protons plus neutrons.
  • Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.

Atoms of one element with different numbers of neutrons are called isotopes. Carbon-12 has 6 neutrons and carbon-14 has 8. Both are carbon.

3The Bohr model

In 1913 Niels Bohr proposed that electrons travel in fixed shells, or energy levels, around the nucleus. For the first twenty elements the shells fill in order:

ShellHolds up to
1st2 electrons
2nd8 electrons
3rd8 electrons
4ththe rest (for elements 19 and 20)

We write the result as an electron arrangement. Sodium, with 11 electrons, is 2, 8, 1. The electrons in the outermost shell are valence electrons. They decide how an atom behaves chemically.

4A model that kept improving

YearScientistIdeaEvidence
about 400 BCDemocritusMatter is made of uncuttable "atomos"Reasoning only
1803John DaltonEach element has its own kind of solid atomMass ratios in reactions
1897J. J. ThomsonAtoms contain tiny negative electronsCathode-ray experiments
1911Ernest RutherfordA tiny, dense, positive nucleusGold-foil experiment
1913Niels BohrElectrons occupy fixed shellsColours of light given off by hydrogen

Rutherford fired positive particles at thin gold foil. Most went straight through, so the atom is mostly empty. A few bounced back, so something small, heavy and positive sits at the centre. Scientists now describe electrons as a cloud, but the Bohr model is still the best first picture.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

An atom of chlorine has atomic number 17 and mass number 35. Find its protons, neutrons and electrons, and its electron arrangement.

  1. Protons = atomic number = 17.
  2. Neutrons = 35 − 17 = 18.
  3. A neutral atom has as many electrons as protons: 17.
  4. Fill the shells: 2, then 8, leaving 7.

Answer: 17 protons, 18 neutrons, 17 electrons, arranged 2, 8, 7

Hands-on: Build-an-Atom Plate

You need: paper plates, three colours of small sweets, beads or beans, a pair of compasses, a pen

  1. Draw a small circle in the centre of a plate for the nucleus and three rings around it for shells.
  2. Choose one colour for protons, one for neutrons and one for electrons.
  3. Build carbon-12: 6 protons and 6 neutrons in the nucleus, electrons 2, 4.
  4. Build oxygen-16, sodium-23 and argon-40. Write the electron arrangement under each.
  5. Which of your atoms has a completely full outer shell? Remember it for next week.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Which particle decides what element an atom is?

    Answer and why

    The proton

    WhyThe number of protons is the atomic number, and each element has its own atomic number.

  2. An atom has 8 protons, 8 neutrons and 8 electrons. What are its atomic number and mass number?

    Answer and why

    Atomic number 8, mass number 16 (it is oxygen)

    WhyAtomic number counts protons. Mass number adds protons and neutrons: 8 + 8.

  3. Write the electron arrangement of calcium, atomic number 20.

    Answer and why

    2, 8, 8, 2

    WhyFill shells in order: 2, then 8, then 8 makes 18, leaving 2 for the fourth shell.

  4. Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes. What is the same and what is different?

    Answer and why

    Same: 6 protons (and 6 electrons). Different: 6 neutrons against 8.

    WhyIsotopes are atoms of one element, so the proton number is fixed. Only the neutron count, and therefore the mass, changes.

  5. In Rutherford's experiment most particles went straight through the foil. What did that show?

    Answer and why

    Atoms are mostly empty space.

    WhyIf atoms were solid all the way through, the particles would have been blocked or deflected much more often.

  6. How many valence electrons does sodium (2, 8, 1) have?

    Answer and why

    One

    WhyValence electrons are those in the outermost occupied shell, and sodium's third shell holds a single electron.

★ Scholar's challenge

Potassium has atomic number 19 and its most common atom has mass number 39. Give its neutrons and electron arrangement. Then compare its outer shell with sodium's and predict whether the two elements behave alike.

Answer and why

20 neutrons; arrangement 2, 8, 8, 1. Yes, they behave alike.

Why39 − 19 = 20. Both atoms have a single valence electron, and valence electrons control chemical behaviour. (Both are soft metals that react strongly with water.)

Words to own

The tiny, dense centre of an atom, holding protons and neutrons.
Atomic number
The number of protons in an atom.
Mass number
The total number of protons and neutrons.
Isotope
Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
Valence electron
An electron in the outermost shell.

A simplified picture used to explain and predict.
