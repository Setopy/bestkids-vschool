Latin & Greek Number Roots II

The big questionOctober has the root for eight but is the tenth month. December has the root for ten but is the twelfth. Did the Romans forget how to count?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Recall the Latin and Greek roots for six to ten, one hundred and one thousand.
  2. Use the roots for "half" and "many".
  3. Explain the names of the months September to December.
  4. Explain how the metric prefixes use Greek and Latin differently.

An octopus is "eight-foot": octo- plus pous, the Greek for foot, which also gives -pod. An octave is a span of eight musical notes. A hexapod is a six-footed creature, which is to say an insect.

Per cent means "for each hundred". A mile was once mille passus, a thousand paces of a Roman soldier. A polyglot speaks many tongues. Centipedes do not really have a hundred feet, and millipedes do not have a thousand. The names are exaggerations.

3The mystery of the months

The earliest Roman calendar began in March, when the campaigning and farming year opened. Count from March:

MonthRootPosition counting from March
Septemberseptem, seven7th
Octoberocto, eight8th
Novembernovem, nine9th
Decemberdecem, ten10th

Later the Romans moved the start of the civil year to January, and the names became wrong by two but were never changed. The fifth and sixth months, once Quintilis and Sextilis, were renamed July and August in honour of Julius Caesar and the emperor Augustus.

4The metric secret

Look again at the prefixes from Science in Week 1. The inventors of the metric system used a deliberate rule:

Bigger than the unit: GreekSmaller than the unit: Latin
deca- (× 10)deci- (÷ 10)
hecto- (× 100)centi- (÷ 100)
kilo- (× 1,000)milli- (÷ 1,000)

So a kilometre is a thousand metres, while a millimetre is a thousandth of a metre. Both prefixes mean "thousand". The language tells you whether to multiply or divide.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Decode "hemisphere", "polygon" and "centimetre".

  1. hemi- is Greek for half, and sphere is a ball. Half a ball.
  2. poly- is Greek for many, and -gon is angle. A shape with many angles.
  3. centi- is Latin, so it divides: a hundredth of a metre.

Answer: Half of a sphere; a many-angled shape; one hundredth of a metre.

Hands-on: The Root Calendar and Polygon Zoo

You need: paper, ruler, coloured pencils

  1. Draw the old Roman year as a circle starting in March. Label each month with its number and, where it has one, its root.
  2. Draw and label a triangle, quadrilateral, pentagon, hexagon, heptagon, octagon, nonagon and decagon.
  3. Under each polygon write the Latin and Greek roots for its number.
  4. Make a metric ladder from kilo- down to milli-. Colour the Greek rungs one colour and the Latin rungs another.
  5. Invent three imaginary animals using number roots and -pod or -ped, and draw them.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. How many sides does a heptagon have, and which language is the root from?

    Answer and why

    Seven sides. Greek.

    Whyhepta- is the Greek root for seven. The Latin root is sept-.

  2. Why is October the tenth month when octo means eight?

    Answer and why

    The Roman year originally began in March, making October the eighth month.

    WhyThe start of the year was later moved to January, but the old names were kept.

  3. What does "per cent" literally mean?

    Answer and why

    "For each hundred"

    WhyLatin per means for each and centum means hundred, so 25 per cent is 25 for each hundred.

  4. Which is bigger, a hectolitre or a centilitre? How do you know from the language?

    Answer and why

    A hectolitre

    WhyGreek prefixes multiply: hecto- is 100 litres. Latin prefixes divide: centi- is a hundredth of a litre.

  5. What is a polyglot?

    Answer and why

    A person who speaks many languages.

    Whypoly- means many and glotta is Greek for tongue.

  6. How many years are in a decade, a century and a millennium?

    Answer and why

    10, 100 and 1,000

    WhyThe roots are dec- (ten), cent- (hundred) and mill- (thousand).

★ Scholar's challenge

A decathlon has ten events and a heptathlon has seven. Build correct names, from Greek roots, for contests with two, three, five and eight events. Which of them really exist?

Answer and why

Biathlon, triathlon, pentathlon and octathlon. The first three are well-known sports.

WhyThe contest root is -athlon. "Biathlon" breaks the pattern by using the Latin bi- where Greek would give di-: real languages are not perfectly tidy. An octathlon exists in some youth athletics but is rare.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • The month names have been "wrong" for over two thousand years. Should they be corrected? Why do people keep old names?
  • Is it helpful or confusing that English has both "semi-" and "hemi-" for half?
  • If you were naming a new unit a million times smaller than a metre, how would you decide which language to use?
