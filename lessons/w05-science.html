⚛ Science · Week 5

Molecular Bonds

The big questionSodium is a metal that explodes in water. Chlorine is a poisonous green gas. Join them and you get table salt, which you sprinkle on your dinner. How can two dangerous things make something safe?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Explain why atoms form bonds.
  2. Describe ionic bonding as a transfer of electrons and covalent bonding as a sharing of electrons.
  3. Tell an element from a compound and an atom from a molecule.
  4. Read a chemical formula and count its atoms.

1Why atoms bond

Last week you saw that the noble gases have full outer shells and almost never react. A full outer shell is a stable arrangement. Other atoms reach it by giving away, taking or sharing electrons. When they do, they become held together by a chemical bond.

A substance made of two or more different elements bonded together is a compound. A compound's properties can be completely unlike those of its elements. That is why salt is nothing like sodium or chlorine.

2Ionic bonds: give and take

Sodium is 2, 8, 1. Losing one electron leaves a full shell: 2, 8. Chlorine is 2, 8, 7. Gaining one electron fills its shell: 2, 8, 8.

So sodium hands its outer electron to chlorine. Each atom now has unequal numbers of protons and electrons, so it carries a charge. A charged atom is an ion.

AtomDoes whatBecomes
Sodium, NaLoses 1 electronNa⁺, a positive ion
Chlorine, ClGains 1 electronCl⁻, a negative ion

Opposite charges attract. That attraction is an ionic bond. Ionic bonds form between a metal and a non-metal. The ions stack in a regular grid called a crystal lattice, which is why salt grains are tiny cubes.

3Covalent bonds: sharing

Two non-metals both want to gain electrons, so neither will give one up. They share. A shared pair of electrons is a covalent bond, and a group of atoms joined this way is a molecule.

Oxygen is 2, 6 and needs two more electrons. Hydrogen has one and needs one more. One oxygen shares a pair with each of two hydrogens. All three atoms now have full outer shells. The result is water, H₂O.

Ionic compoundsCovalent compounds
Formed betweenMetal and non-metalNon-metals
Electrons areTransferredShared

ExamplesNaCl, MgOH₂O, CO₂, CH₄

4Reading a formula

A chemical formula lists the elements in a compound and how many atoms of each. The small number written after a symbol is a subscript. No subscript means one atom.

FormulaNameAtoms
H₂OWater2 hydrogen, 1 oxygen
CO₂Carbon dioxide1 carbon, 2 oxygen
CH₄Methane1 carbon, 4 hydrogen
NaClSodium chloride1 sodium, 1 chlorine
C₆H₁₂O₆Glucose6 carbon, 12 hydrogen, 6 oxygen

Some elements travel as molecules of two identical atoms, such as oxygen gas, O₂. That is a molecule but not a compound, because only one element is present.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Magnesium is 2, 8, 2 and oxygen is 2, 6. What kind of bond forms, and what ions result?

  1. Magnesium is a metal and oxygen a non-metal, so the bond is ionic.
  2. Magnesium loses 2 electrons to reach 2, 8. It becomes Mg²⁺.
  3. Oxygen gains 2 electrons to reach 2, 8. It becomes O²⁻.
  4. One magnesium supplies exactly what one oxygen needs.

Answer: An ionic bond, forming magnesium oxide, MgO

Example 2

How many atoms are in one molecule of sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄?

  1. H₂ means 2 hydrogen atoms.
  2. S has no subscript: 1 sulfur atom.
  3. O₄ means 4 oxygen atoms.

Answer: 2 + 1 + 4 = 7 atoms

Hands-on: Sweet Molecules and Salt Crystals

You need: soft sweets or clay balls in three colours, toothpicks, table salt, a magnifying glass, a dark plate, warm water

  1. Choose a colour each for hydrogen, oxygen and carbon. Build H₂O, CO₂, CH₄ and O₂ with toothpicks as bonds.
  2. For each model, say which atoms are sharing and how many bonds each atom has made.
  3. Sprinkle salt on the dark plate and look through the magnifier. Draw the shape of the grains.
  4. Dissolve two spoonfuls of salt in a little warm water and leave it in a sunny place for several days.
  5. Examine the new crystals. Why do they always form the same shape?

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Why do atoms form bonds?

    Answer and why

    To reach a stable, full outer shell of electrons.

    WhyAtoms with incomplete outer shells become more stable by losing, gaining or sharing electrons.

  2. What is an ion?

    Answer and why

    An atom that has gained or lost electrons and so carries an electric charge.

    WhyIts protons and electrons are no longer equal in number, so the charges no longer cancel.

  3. Which type of bond forms between potassium (a metal) and fluorine (a non-metal)?

    Answer and why

    An ionic bond

    WhyThe metal gives up its outer electron and the non-metal takes it, making oppositely charged ions that attract.

  4. How many atoms of each element are in glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, and how many altogether?

    Answer and why

    6 carbon, 12 hydrogen, 6 oxygen: 24 atoms

    WhyEach subscript counts the atoms of the element just before it: 6 + 12 + 6.

  5. Is O₂ a compound? Explain.

    Answer and why

    No. It is a molecule of an element.

    WhyA compound must contain at least two different elements. O₂ contains only oxygen.

  6. Why does table salt have a high melting point?

    Answer and why

    Its ions are held in a lattice by strong attractions in every direction.

    WhyA great deal of energy is needed to pull so many strongly attracted ions apart.

★ Scholar's challenge

Magnesium forms the ion Mg²⁺ and chlorine forms Cl⁻. Work out the formula of magnesium chloride and explain your reasoning.

Answer and why

MgCl₂

WhyMagnesium must lose two electrons, but each chlorine atom can accept only one. Two chlorine atoms are needed for each magnesium, so the charges balance: +2 and two of −1.

Words to own

Chemical bond
A force that holds atoms together.
Ion
An atom with an electric charge from gaining or losing electrons.
Ionic bond
Attraction between oppositely charged ions.
Covalent bond
A bond formed when atoms share a pair of electrons.
Molecule
Two or more atoms joined by covalent bonds.
Compound
A substance made of two or more different elements bonded together.
