🏛 History & Geography · Week 7

Ancient Egypt

The big questionEgypt lasted for almost three thousand years. Cleopatra lived closer in time to us than to the builders of the Great Pyramid. What allowed one civilisation to endure so long?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Explain how the Nile shaped Egyptian life and belief.
  2. Describe the role of the pharaoh and the structure of Egyptian society.
  3. Explain how and why the pyramids were built.
  4. Describe hieroglyphs and how they were deciphered.

1The gift of the Nile

Egypt is a desert with one river running through it. The Nile flows north for about 6,650 km from the heart of Africa to the Mediterranean, where it fans out in a delta. The Greek historian Herodotus called Egypt "the gift of the Nile".

Each summer, rains far to the south made the river rise and flood, on almost the same dates every year. When the water went down it left a strip of rich black silt. Egyptians called this farmland Kemet, the Black Land, and the desert beyond Deshret, the Red Land. Their year had three seasons.

SeasonMonths (approx.)What happened
Akhet (flood)July to OctoberFields under water; farmers worked on royal building projects
Peret (growing)November to FebruaryPloughing and sowing in the fresh mud
Shemu (harvest)March to JuneReaping and storing grain

Compare this with Mesopotamia, where floods were violent and unpredictable. Egypt's river was regular, and the deserts and sea protected it from invasion. Egyptians came to see the world as orderly and lasting. Their word for this right order, truth and balance was ma'at.

Because the Nile flows north, the south is called Upper Egypt (upstream) and the north Lower Egypt (downstream).

2The pharaoh

About 3100 BC a king traditionally called Narmer, or Menes, united Upper and Lower Egypt. The ruler, later called pharaoh, wore a double crown and was believed to be a living god, responsible for keeping ma'at. He owned the land, commanded the army and directed the storing of grain against bad years.

RankWho
1Pharaoh
2Vizier (chief minister), priests, nobles
3Scribes and officials
4Craftspeople and merchants
5Farmers, the great majority
6Servants and enslaved people

Historians divide Egypt's history into the Old Kingdom (the age of pyramids), the Middle Kingdom and the New Kingdom (the age of empire, with rulers such as Hatshepsut, Tutankhamun and Ramses II).

3Houses of eternity

Egyptians believed life continued after death if the body was preserved and provided for. Bodies were dried and wrapped as mummies, and tombs were filled with food, furniture and treasure.

The pyramids are royal tombs of the Old Kingdom. The Great Pyramid of Giza was built for the pharaoh Khufu about 2560 BC. It contained about 2.3 million stone blocks, stood about 146 m tall, and was the tallest structure on Earth for nearly four thousand years. Its sides are lined up almost perfectly with north, south, east and west.

It was not built by slaves. Archaeologists have found the workers' town, with bakeries, breweries and doctors. The builders were paid, organised crews, joined in the flood season by farmers who owed labour to the king.

4Sacred writing

Egyptians wrote in hieroglyphs, picture signs that could stand for a whole word or for a sound. Scribes wrote with reed pens on papyrus, sheets made from a river reed. Our word "paper" comes from it.

After Egypt fell under Roman and later rule, the knowledge of hieroglyphs was lost for about 1,400 years. In 1799 French soldiers found the Rosetta Stone, which carries one decree in three scripts: hieroglyphic, a later Egyptian script, and Greek. Scholars could read the Greek. In 1822 Jean-François Champollion used it to crack the code, and Egypt could speak again.

5Lasting knowledge

  • A 365-day calendar, based on the flood and the star Sirius.
  • Practical geometry, for re-measuring fields after each flood and for building.
  • Medicine: written manuals on wounds, fractures and remedies.
  • Monumental architecture in stone.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Explain how the Nile's flood helped make the pyramids possible.

  1. The flood renewed the soil every year, giving large and reliable harvests.
  2. The surplus grain was collected and stored by the pharaoh's officials.
  3. During the flood months farmers could not work their fields.
  4. The pharaoh used the stored grain to feed and pay those farmers to build.

Answer: The Nile supplied both the food surplus and the free labour season that great building projects required.

Hands-on: Cartouche and the Pyramid Problem

You need: card, gold or yellow pencil, a hieroglyphic alphabet chart (from a book or online), squared paper

  1. A cartouche is the oval that encloses a royal name. Draw one and write your own name inside it in hieroglyphs, sound by sound.
  2. The Great Pyramid's base is a square about 230 m on each side. Work out the distance all the way round it.
  3. If you walk 1 m each second, how many minutes would that walk take?
  4. If 2.3 million blocks were laid over 20 years, about how many blocks were laid each year? Each day?
  5. Write three sentences on what your results tell you about Egyptian organisation.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Why did Herodotus call Egypt "the gift of the Nile"?

    Answer and why

    Without the river's water and silt, Egypt would be empty desert.

    WhyAll of Egypt's farmland, drinking water and transport came from the one river.

  2. Why is southern Egypt called Upper Egypt?

    Answer and why

    It is upstream. The Nile flows from south to north.

    Why"Upper" refers to higher ground nearer the river's source, not to the top of the map.

  3. What is ma'at?

    Answer and why

    The Egyptian idea of truth, justice, balance and right order.

    WhyThe regular Nile and a secure land encouraged a belief in a stable, ordered world that the pharaoh had to maintain.

  4. Who built the pyramids, and how do we know?

    Answer and why

    Paid, organised workers and seasonal farm labourers, not slaves. Their town has been excavated.

    WhyArchaeologists found housing, bakeries and medical care for the builders near Giza.

  5. Why was the Rosetta Stone the key to hieroglyphs?

    Answer and why

    It gives the same text in hieroglyphs and in Greek, which scholars could already read.

    WhyMatching the known language against the unknown one let Champollion work out the values of the signs.

  6. Why did Egyptians mummify their dead?

    Answer and why

    They believed the body was needed for life after death.

    WhyPreserving the body, and supplying the tomb, was meant to secure the person's survival in the afterlife.

★ Scholar's challenge

Mesopotamia and Egypt both grew up beside rivers, yet Mesopotamia was a land of rival city-states and Egypt was one kingdom for most of 3,000 years. Using geography, give two reasons for the difference.

Answer and why

Egypt had one river acting as a single highway, and natural barriers of desert and sea. Mesopotamia had two rivers, many separate canal systems and open borders.

WhyThe Nile's current carried boats north and the wind blew them south, tying the whole valley together under one ruler, while deserts kept invaders out. Mesopotamian cities competed for water and were exposed on every side.

The king of ancient Egypt, regarded as a god.

The picture-based writing of ancient Egypt.

A reed, and the writing sheets made from it.

The fan of land where a river meets the sea.
