Ω Languages · Week 3

Morphology 101

The big questionYou have probably never seen the word "unmicrowaveable", yet you understood it at once. How can you know the meaning of a word nobody ever taught you?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Define morpheme, root, prefix and suffix.
  2. Split words into their morphemes.
  3. Tell free morphemes from bound ones.
  4. Use common prefixes and suffixes to work out unfamiliar words.

1Words are built from parts

Morphology is the study of how words are built. The smallest piece of a word that carries meaning is a morpheme.

The word unbreakable has three morphemes: un- (not), break, and -able (can be). A morpheme is not the same as a syllable. Elephant has three syllables but only one morpheme, because "el", "e" and "phant" mean nothing alone. Cats has one syllable but two morphemes: cat and -s (more than one).

2Free and bound

  • A free morpheme can stand alone as a word: break, kind, play.
  • A bound morpheme must be attached to something: un-, -ness, -ed, -s.

The root is the core that carries the main meaning. An affix is a bound morpheme added to a root. An affix placed in front is a prefix. One placed behind is a suffix.

4Two kinds of suffix

An inflectional suffix changes the grammar of a word but not what kind of word it is: walk, walks, walked, walking. A derivational affix builds a new word, often of a different kind: kind (adjective) becomes kindness (noun).

Other languages do the same. Spanish -mente matches English -ly (rápidamente, rapidly), and Spanish des- matches dis- (desaparecer, disappear). Morphology is a tool you can carry from language to language.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Split "reconstruction" into morphemes and work out its meaning.

  1. Prefix: re- (again).
  2. Prefix: con- (together).
  3. Root: struct (build), from Latin struere.
  4. Suffix: -ion (action or result).

Answer: re + con + struct + ion: "the act of building together again"

Example 2

How many morphemes are in "carpet"?

  1. Tempting split: car + pet.
  2. Test the meaning: a carpet has nothing to do with a car or a pet.

Answer: One. A piece counts as a morpheme only if it carries its meaning into the word.

Hands-on: The Word Factory

You need: 30 small cards, three colours of pen

  1. Write ten prefixes in one colour, ten roots in another (act, form, port, view, play, kind, hope, read, build, agree) and ten suffixes in the third.
  2. Build as many real words as you can by laying cards side by side. Record each.
  3. Now build five words that do not exist but could, such as "unviewable" or "misbuilder". Write a dictionary definition for each.
  4. Sort your real words: which suffixes were inflectional and which derivational?
  5. Find the longest real word you can build from the cards.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What is a morpheme?

    Answer and why

    The smallest unit of a word that carries meaning.

    WhyIt cannot be cut further without losing meaning, which makes it different from a letter or a syllable.

  2. Split "unhappiness" into morphemes.

    Answer and why

    un + happy + ness (three morphemes)

    Why"un-" means not, "happy" is the root, and "-ness" turns the adjective into a noun. The y changes to i in spelling only.

  3. How many morphemes are in "teachers"?

    Answer and why

    Three: teach + er + s

    Why"teach" is the root, "-er" makes a person who does it, and "-s" makes it plural.

  4. Is "-ed" in "walked" free or bound? Inflectional or derivational?

    Answer and why

    Bound and inflectional

    WhyIt cannot stand alone, and it changes only the tense. "Walked" is still a verb.

  5. Use morphemes to explain "transatlantic".

    Answer and why

    "Across the Atlantic"

    Why"trans-" means across, so a transatlantic flight crosses the Atlantic Ocean.

  6. "Disagreement" contains which root, prefix and suffix?

    Answer and why

    Prefix dis-, root agree, suffix -ment

    Why"dis-" reverses the meaning and "-ment" turns the verb into a noun: the state of not agreeing.

★ Scholar's challenge

Split "internationalisation" into morphemes and explain how each one changes the word.

Answer and why

inter + nation + al + ise + ation (five morphemes)

Why"nation" is the root. "-al" makes an adjective (national). "inter-" adds "between". "-ise" makes a verb (to make international). "-ation" makes a noun (the process of doing so).

Words to own

Morphology
The study of how words are formed from meaningful parts.
Morpheme
The smallest meaningful part of a word.
Root
The core morpheme carrying a word's main meaning.
Affix
A bound morpheme attached to a root; a prefix or suffix.
Inflection
A change to a word that shows grammar, such as tense or number.
Derivation
Building a new word by adding an affix.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Is "unmicrowaveable" a real word if no dictionary lists it? What makes a word real?
  • Why might it be faster to learn fifty roots than five hundred separate words?
  • Some languages build one enormous word where English uses a whole sentence. Which system seems easier to you, and why?
