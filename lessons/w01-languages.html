Ω Languages · Week 1

The Indo-European Tree

The big questionThe word for "mother" is madre in Spanish, Mutter in German, mātṛ in ancient Sanskrit and mat' in Russian. These peoples lived thousands of kilometres apart. Coincidence?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Explain what a language family is and how languages descend from a common ancestor.
  2. Name the main branches of the Indo-European family.
  3. Recognise cognates across languages.
  4. Name languages that belong to other families.

1Languages have families

Languages change slowly, all the time. When a group of speakers splits and the halves stop talking to each other, their speech drifts apart. After a thousand years or more the two groups can no longer understand each other. One language has become two daughter languages.

Latin was spoken across the Roman Empire. When the empire broke up, the Latin of each region drifted. Today we call the results Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese and Romanian. They are sisters, and Latin is their mother.

2The great tree

In 1786 Sir William Jones, a judge in India, noticed that Sanskrit, Greek and Latin were too alike for chance. Scholars concluded that most languages of Europe, Iran and northern India descend from one lost language. We call it Proto-Indo-European, or PIE. It was spoken perhaps 5,000 to 6,000 years ago, probably on the grasslands north of the Black Sea. Nobody wrote it down. Linguists rebuilt it by comparing its descendants.

Today nearly half of all people speak an Indo-European language as their first language.

3Cognates: family resemblance

Cognates are words in different languages that grew from the same ancestor word.

Sound changes follow rules. Where Latin has p, English often has f: pater and father, pes/pedis and foot, piscis and fish. This pattern is part of Grimm's Law, worked out by Jacob Grimm, the same man who collected the fairy tales.

4Not every language is on this tree

Many of the world's languages belong to other families entirely: Mandarin (Sino-Tibetan), Arabic and Hebrew (Afro-Asiatic), Swahili and Yoruba (Niger-Congo), Thai (Kra-Dai), Nahuatl, the language of the Aztecs (Uto-Aztecan), and Finnish and Hungarian (Uralic). Basque, in Spain and France, has no known relatives at all.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Are English "fish" and Spanish "pez" cognates?

  1. Find the Latin: piscis.
  2. Spanish descends from Latin: piscis became pez.
  3. English is Germanic. Apply Grimm's Law: Latin p matches English f, so pisc- matches fisc-, which became "fish".

Answer: Yes. Both descend from the same PIE word. They are cousins, not borrowed copies.

Hands-on: Grow the Tree

You need: large sheet of paper, coloured pens, a dictionary or translation tool

  1. Draw a thick trunk labelled Proto-Indo-European and six large branches for the six groups in the table.
  2. Hang at least three languages on each branch as leaves.
  3. Choose the words two, star and name. Find each in six languages from at least three branches, and write them along the branches.
  4. Circle the sounds that stay the same in one colour and the sounds that change in another.
  5. Off to the side, draw three separate small trees for three non-Indo-European languages.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What is a language family?

    Answer and why

    A group of languages descended from one common ancestor language.

    WhyLanguages drift when speakers separate, so related languages keep shared words and patterns, just as relatives share features.

  2. Name the mother language of Spanish, French and Italian.

    Answer and why

    Latin

    WhyThese grew from the everyday Latin spoken in different regions of the Roman Empire after it broke apart.

  3. To which branch does English belong?

    Answer and why

    Germanic

    WhyIts core words (mother, water, house, three) match German and Dutch. English later borrowed many words from French and Latin, but its skeleton is Germanic.

  4. Latin pater and English "father" are cognates. Which rule explains the different first letter?

    Answer and why

    Grimm's Law: PIE p became f in the Germanic languages.

    WhyThe change is regular, and appears again in pes/foot and piscis/fish. Regular patterns are how linguists prove kinship.

  5. Is Mandarin an Indo-European language?

    Answer and why

    No. It belongs to the Sino-Tibetan family.

    WhyIts basic words and grammar show no regular match with Indo-European languages.

  6. Nobody ever wrote Proto-Indo-European. How can we know anything about it?

    Answer and why

    By comparing its daughter languages and working backwards.

    WhyWhen many related languages share a word with regular sound differences, linguists can reconstruct the likely original, like rebuilding a grandparent's face from the grandchildren.

★ Scholar's challenge

English has both "foot" and "pedal", both "father" and "paternal", both "fish" and "Pisces". Why does English often have two related words, one with f and one with p?

Answer and why

One is the inherited Germanic word, and the other was borrowed later from Latin.

WhyThe inherited words went through Grimm's Law (p to f). The Latin borrowings arrived after that change was finished, so they kept their p.
