Bar Modeling I: Part-Whole and Comparison

The big questionPupils in Singapore are among the best problem-solvers in the world, and one of their main tools is a simple rectangle. How can drawing a bar make a hard word problem easy?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Draw a part-whole bar model for a word problem.
  2. Draw a comparison model for "more than", "fewer than" and "times as many".
  3. Find the value of one unit and use it to answer the question.
  4. Check every answer against the story.

1Why draw?

Most mistakes in word problems happen before any arithmetic: the solver has not understood how the quantities are related. A bar model turns the words into a picture. Each quantity becomes a rectangle whose length shows its size. Once the picture is right, the calculation is usually obvious.

This is the middle step of the Singapore method: concrete objects, then pictorial models, then abstract symbols.

2The part-whole model

Use it when a whole is made of parts. Draw one bar, split it into its parts, and mark what is known and what is unknown with a question mark.

whole = part + part, and part = whole − other part.

3The comparison model

Use it when two quantities are compared. Draw one bar for each, lined up on the left, so the difference can be seen.

For "times as many", draw equal units. If Maya has 3 times as many books as Tom, Tom is 1 unit and Maya is 3 units.

The key move is always the same: find the value of one unit.

4The four-step routine

  • Read the whole problem twice. Say what is being asked.
  • Draw the bars and label every known number. Put a question mark on the unknown.
  • Compute from the picture.
  • Check by putting your answer back into the story.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

A school has 1,250 pupils. 585 are boys. How many more girls than boys are there?

  1. Part-whole: girls = 1,250 − 585 = 665.
  2. Comparison: difference = 665 − 585 = 80.
  3. Check: 585 + 665 = 1,250. ✓

Answer: There are 80 more girls than boys.

Example 2

Maya has 3 times as many books as Tom. Together they have 96 books. How many does each have?

  1. Tom is 1 unit and Maya is 3 units, so together they are 4 units.
  2. 4 units = 96, so 1 unit = 96 ÷ 4 = 24.
  3. Tom has 24. Maya has 3 × 24 = 72.
  4. Check: 24 + 72 = 96, and 72 is 3 times 24. ✓

Answer: Tom has 24 books and Maya has 72.

Example 3

A rope 84 m long is cut into two pieces. One piece is 18 m longer than the other. How long is the shorter piece?

  1. Draw two bars. The longer one is the shorter one plus an extra 18.
  2. Remove the extra 18: 84 − 18 = 66. What remains is two equal bars.
  3. 66 ÷ 2 = 33.
  4. Check: shorter 33, longer 51, and 33 + 51 = 84. ✓

Answer: The shorter piece is 33 m.

Hands-on: Paper-Strip Models

You need: strips of coloured paper, scissors, glue, a notebook

  1. Cut strips to stand for 1 unit. Use them to build the Maya and Tom problem on the table.
  2. Write three word problems of your own: one part-whole, one "more than", and one "times as many".
  3. Swap with a parent. Each of you must build the model in paper strips before writing any numbers.
  4. Glue the best model in your notebook and write the solution beside it.
  5. Explain aloud: what does one strip stand for in each problem?

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Juan saved $320. He spent $145 on a bicycle and $68 on a helmet. How much does he have left?

    Answer and why

    $107

    WhyThe whole is 320 and the parts are 145, 68 and the unknown. 145 + 68 = 213, and 320 − 213 = 107.

  2. Sara has 5 times as many beads as Li. Sara has 64 more beads than Li. How many does Li have?

    Answer and why

    16 beads

    WhyLi is 1 unit and Sara is 5 units, so the difference is 4 units. 4 units = 64, so 1 unit = 16. (Sara has 80.)

  3. A tank holds 3 times as much water as a bucket. Together they hold 48 L. How much does the tank hold?

    Answer and why

    36 L

    WhyBucket 1 unit, tank 3 units, total 4 units = 48 L. One unit is 12 L, so the tank is 36 L.

  4. Ana and Beto have 130 marbles altogether. Ana has 20 fewer than Beto. How many does Beto have?

    Answer and why

    75 marbles

    WhyGive Ana 20 more and the bars would be equal: 130 + 20 = 150, and 150 ÷ 2 = 75. Check: Ana has 55, and 55 + 75 = 130.

  5. The sum of two numbers is 150. One number is 4 times the other. Find both.

    Answer and why

    30 and 120

    Why1 unit plus 4 units is 5 units = 150, so 1 unit = 30 and 4 units = 120.

  6. A farmer has 245 hens and 178 more ducks than hens. How many birds does he have altogether?

    Answer and why

    668 birds

    WhyDucks = 245 + 178 = 423. Total = 245 + 423 = 668. The comparison bar shows the hens counted twice plus the extra 178.

★ Scholar's challenge

Three children share 180 stickers. Bela has twice as many as Aldo. Cira has 20 more than Bela. How many does each child have?

Answer and why

Aldo 32, Bela 64, Cira 84

WhyAldo is 1 unit, Bela 2 units, Cira 2 units plus 20. Remove the extra 20: 160 is 5 units, so 1 unit = 32. Check: 32 + 64 + 84 = 180.

Words to own

Bar model
A drawing that uses rectangles to show how quantities are related.
Part-whole
A relationship in which parts add up to a total.
Comparison
A relationship showing how much larger one quantity is than another.
Unit
One of the equal pieces in a bar model.
Difference
How much more one quantity is than another.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Why might a picture help your brain more than a sentence does?
  • Is drawing a bar model "real" mathematics, or only a crutch until you learn algebra?
  • When you check an answer against the story, what kinds of mistakes can you catch that arithmetic alone would miss?
