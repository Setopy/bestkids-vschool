∑ Mathematics · Week 3

Order of Operations

The big questionType 8 + 4 × 3 into a cheap calculator and you may get 36. A scientific calculator gives 20. Both machines can add and multiply perfectly. So which one is wrong, and who says so?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. State the agreed order of operations and explain why an agreement is needed.
  2. Evaluate expressions with brackets, exponents and all four operations.
  3. Work multiplication and division, and addition and subtraction, from left to right.
  4. Insert brackets to make a statement true.

1Why we need an agreement

An expression such as 8 + 4 × 3 is a sentence in the language of mathematics. If readers could do the steps in any order, the same sentence would have several meanings. Mathematicians everywhere therefore follow one convention, an agreed rule.

The rule is not random. Multiplication is repeated addition, so 4 × 3 is a packed bundle (4 + 4 + 4) that must be opened before joining it to anything else. Exponents are repeated multiplication, so they are packed even tighter.

2The order

StepOperationNote
1BracketsInnermost first
2ExponentsPowers such as 5²
3Multiplication and divisionEqual rank: work left to right
4Addition and subtractionEqual rank: work left to right

You may know a memory word such as BODMAS, BIDMAS or PEMDAS. Be careful: the word makes it look as if division comes before multiplication, or addition before subtraction. They do not. Partners of equal rank are done in the order they appear, from left to right.

So 36 ÷ 6 × 3 is (36 ÷ 6) × 3 = 18, not 36 ÷ 18 = 2. And 20 − 5 + 3 is 15 + 3 = 18, not 20 − 8 = 12.

3Writing a clean solution

Rewrite the whole expression on a new line after every step, changing only one thing at a time. Keep the equals signs lined up. This habit makes mistakes easy to find and is how professional mathematicians write.

A fraction bar acts like brackets: everything above it is one group and everything below is another.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Evaluate 5 + 2 × (9 − 4)².

  1. Brackets: 9 − 4 = 5, giving 5 + 2 × 5².
  2. Exponent: 5² = 25, giving 5 + 2 × 25.
  3. Multiplication: 2 × 25 = 50, giving 5 + 50.
  4. Addition: 55.

Answer: 55

Example 2

Evaluate 48 ÷ (2 + 6) × 3 − 4.

  1. Brackets: 2 + 6 = 8, giving 48 ÷ 8 × 3 − 4.
  2. Division and multiplication, left to right: 48 ÷ 8 = 6, then 6 × 3 = 18.
  3. Subtraction: 18 − 4 = 14.

Answer: 14

Example 3

Insert one pair of brackets to make 4 + 6 × 2 − 1 = 19 true.

  1. Without brackets: 4 + 12 − 1 = 15. Too small.
  2. We need the addition to happen first: (4 + 6) × 2 − 1.
  3. Check: 10 × 2 − 1 = 19. ✓

Answer: (4 + 6) × 2 − 1 = 19

Hands-on: Four Fours

You need: paper, pencil, a timer

  1. Using exactly four 4s and any operations and brackets, make each whole number from 0 to 10.
  2. Start with these: 44 ÷ 44 = 1 and (4 + 4 + 4) ÷ 4 = 3.
  3. Write every expression with correct brackets, then hand it to a partner to evaluate without help.
  4. If your partner gets a different answer, find out whose order of operations slipped.
  5. Extension: how far beyond 10 can you go?

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Evaluate 7 × 8 − 6 × 5.

    Answer and why

    26

    WhyBoth multiplications come before the subtraction: 56 − 30 = 26.

  2. Evaluate 20 − 12 ÷ 4 + 3.

    Answer and why

    20

    WhyDivision first: 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Then left to right: 20 − 3 = 17, and 17 + 3 = 20.

  3. Evaluate 2³ + 4² ÷ 8.

    Answer and why

    10

    WhyExponents first: 8 + 16 ÷ 8. Then division: 8 + 2. Then addition: 10.

  4. Evaluate 100 − 4 × (3 + 2)².

    Answer and why

    0

    WhyBrackets give 5, the exponent gives 25, the multiplication gives 100, and 100 − 100 = 0.

  5. Evaluate 36 ÷ 6 × 3.

    Answer and why

    18

    WhyDivision and multiplication have equal rank, so work left to right: 6 × 3 = 18.

  6. Evaluate [18 − (4 + 2) × 2] ÷ 3.

    Answer and why

    2

    WhyInnermost brackets: 6. Inside the square brackets multiply first: 18 − 12 = 6. Then 6 ÷ 3 = 2.

  7. Insert one pair of brackets to make 3 + 5 × 4 − 2 = 13 true.

    Answer and why

    3 + 5 × (4 − 2) = 13

    WhyThe brackets force the subtraction first: 5 × 2 = 10, and 3 + 10 = 13.

★ Scholar's challenge

Using the digits 2, 3, 4 and 5 once each, in that order, with any operations and brackets, make 14. Then make 25. Then make 45.

Answer and why

2 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 14; (2 + 3) × 4 + 5 = 25; (2 + 3) × (4 + 5) = 45

WhyFor 25, the brackets make the addition happen first: 5 × 4 + 5 = 25. For 45, two pairs of brackets give 5 × 9. The same four digits give very different values depending only on the order of the operations.

Words to own

Expression
Numbers and operations written together, with no equals sign.
Evaluate
To work out the value of an expression.
Convention
A rule everyone agrees to follow so that meaning is shared.
Exponent
A raised number that tells how many times to multiply the base by itself.
Brackets
Symbols that group part of an expression to be done first.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • The order of operations is an agreement, not a law of nature. Could a different agreement work just as well?
  • Why is "left to right" needed for subtraction and division but not for addition and multiplication?
  • Where else in life do people need conventions so that everyone reads the same meaning?
