✒ Literature · Week 2

The Hero's Journey

The big questionA shepherd boy, a farm girl and a small creature who loves his armchair all leave home, face a great danger and return changed. Why do stories from every corner of the world follow the same road?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Name the main stages of the Hero's Journey.
  2. Identify those stages in a story.
  3. Recognise the archetypes of hero, mentor, herald and shadow.
  4. Explain why the hero must return changed.

1One story, a thousand faces

In 1949 the scholar Joseph Campbell compared myths from many cultures and found a shared pattern. He called it the monomyth. We know it as the Hero's Journey. It has three great movements: Departure, Initiation and Return.

Storytellers did not copy each other. The pattern keeps appearing because it mirrors growing up: we leave what is safe, are tested, and come back as someone new.

2The road, stage by stage

StageWhat happens
1. Ordinary WorldWe meet the hero at home, before anything changes.
2. Call to AdventureA problem or invitation arrives.
3. Refusal of the CallThe hero hesitates out of fear or duty.
4. Meeting the MentorA wiser figure gives advice, training or a gift.
5. Crossing the ThresholdThe hero leaves the known world. No turning back.
6. Tests, Allies, EnemiesTrials teach the rules of the new world.
7. The OrdealThe greatest danger. The hero faces death or deepest fear.
8. The RewardThe hero wins something: an object, knowledge, or courage.
9. The Road BackThe journey home, often with a final chase or test.
10. Return with the GiftThe hero comes home changed and shares what was won.

Not every story uses every stage, and the order can bend. The pattern is a map, not a cage.

3Archetypes

An archetype is a kind of character that appears again and again across stories.

  • Hero: the one who must change.
  • Mentor: the guide, who often cannot finish the journey with the hero.
  • Herald: the one who brings the call.
  • Threshold guardian: someone who blocks the way and tests whether the hero is ready.
  • Shadow: the great enemy, often a dark mirror of what the hero could become.

4Why the return matters

A hero who wins and keeps the prize is only an adventurer. The journey is complete when the hero brings something home that heals the community. The outward journey always stands for an inward one: the dragon outside is the fear inside.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Map this tale: "Lina, a baker's daughter, has never left her village. When the river dries up, an old traveller says its source is blocked high in the mountains. Lina says she is only a baker. But the traveller gives her a brass compass, and she sets out. She crosses the pass, outwits a toll-keeper, befriends a goatherd, and at the source faces the landslide alone at night. She frees the water, nearly drowning. She follows the stream home, and teaches the village to guard the source."

  1. Ordinary World: Lina in the village bakery.
  2. Call: the river dries up. Herald and mentor: the old traveller.
  3. Refusal: "I am only a baker."
  4. Mentor's gift: the brass compass. Threshold: crossing the pass.
  5. Tests, allies, enemies: the toll-keeper (a threshold guardian) and the goatherd (an ally).
  6. Ordeal: the landslide at night, nearly drowning. Reward: the freed water.
  7. Road Back and Return with the Gift: she follows the stream home and teaches the village.

Answer: Every major stage is present. Lina returns with water and with knowledge, and she is no longer "only a baker".

Hands-on: Chart a Journey You Know

You need: a favourite book or film, a large sheet of paper

  1. Draw a big circle. Mark "Home" at the top. The upper half is the known world and the lower half is the unknown.
  2. Place the ten stages round the circle, clockwise.
  3. Beside each stage write the matching moment from your story in a few words.
  4. Label any archetypes you can find.
  5. Mark any stage that is missing or out of order. Why might the author have changed the pattern?

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What are the three great movements of the Hero's Journey?

    Answer and why

    Departure, Initiation and Return

    WhyThe hero leaves the known world, is tested and transformed in the unknown, and comes back with something of value.

  2. Why do heroes often refuse the call at first?

    Answer and why

    Fear, doubt or duty holds them back.

    WhyThe refusal shows the danger is real and the hero is ordinary, which makes the later courage mean something.

  3. What is the mentor's job?

    Answer and why

    To prepare the hero with advice, training or a gift.

    WhyThe hero begins unready. The mentor supplies what is missing but usually steps aside so the hero must face the ordeal alone.

  4. What is an archetype?

    Answer and why

    A character type that recurs across many stories and cultures.

    WhyShared human experiences produce shared kinds of characters: the guide, the enemy, the messenger.

  5. In the tale of Lina, what is the "gift" she returns with?

    Answer and why

    The flowing river, and the knowledge of how to protect its source.

    WhyThe gift heals the community's original problem, which is what completes the journey.

  6. Why is the Ordeal placed near the middle or end, not at the start?

    Answer and why

    The hero must first gain skills, allies and courage through earlier tests.

    WhyA victory only convinces us if we have watched the hero earn the strength to win it.

★ Scholar's challenge

Invent a hero's journey in exactly ten sentences, one for each stage. Your hero must be an ordinary person from your own town, and the "gift" must be something other than treasure.

Answer and why

Answers will vary. Check that each sentence matches its stage and that the hero has changed by the end.

WhyCompressing the pattern into ten sentences proves you understand what each stage is for. The best gifts are knowledge, courage or healing, because they can be shared.

Words to own

Monomyth
Campbell's name for the single story pattern shared by many myths.
Archetype
A recurring character type or symbol.
Mentor
A wise guide who prepares the hero.
Threshold
The boundary between the known and unknown worlds.
Ordeal
The hero's greatest test.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Can a hero's journey happen without leaving home? What would the "unknown world" be?
  • Is the shadow always evil, or can it be a part of the hero?
  • Think of a real person you admire. Did their life follow this road?
