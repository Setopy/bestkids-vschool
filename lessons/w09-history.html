🏛 History & Geography · Week 9

Geographic Determinism

The big questionThe first civilisations all arose in river valleys within a narrow band of latitude. Was that because of the land, or because of the people? How much of history is decided by a map?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Explain the idea of geographic determinism and the opposing idea of possibilism.
  2. Compare Mesopotamia, Egypt and the Indus Valley using geographic evidence.
  3. Evaluate the strengths and dangers of geographic explanations.
  4. Write a thesis supported by evidence and tested against a counter-argument.

1The claim

Geographic determinism is the view that the physical environment, meaning climate, landforms, rivers, plants and animals, decides how human societies develop.

The opposing view is possibilism: geography sets limits and offers opportunities, but people choose among them, using their ideas, culture and technology. Most historians today hold a position between the two. Geography shapes history but does not dictate it.

In favour of determinism: all three began on fertile floodplains in dry lands, where irrigation gave a surplus (Weeks 4 and 5). Egypt's shelter and steady river match its unity and confidence. Mesopotamia's exposure and wild floods match its wars and worries (Weeks 6 and 7).

Against determinism: the three rivers offered much the same gifts, yet one people built pyramids for kings and another built drains for citizens (Week 8). Many other river valleys produced no early civilisation at all. And the same Nile flowed past Egypt for thousands of years before the pyramids, and still flows now that they are ruins.

3A larger pattern

The scientist Jared Diamond argued in 1997 that the continents were unequal from the start. Eurasia happened to have more wild plants and animals suitable for domestication, such as wheat, barley, cattle, sheep and horses. It also stretches east to west along similar latitudes, so crops, animals and inventions could spread across the same climates and biomes (Week 3). The Americas and Africa stretch north to south, across many biomes, which made spreading harder.

On this view, differences between peoples come from differences in their starting materials, not from differences in ability. Critics reply that the theory leaves too little room for human decisions, ideas, leaders and chance.

4A warning from history

In the 1800s some writers used a crude determinism to claim that people from hot climates were lazy and that people from cool climates were born to rule. They used it to excuse conquest and slavery. The claim was false, and its results were cruel. The great early civilisations you have studied, in Iraq, Egypt, Pakistan and Mexico, all lay in hot lands.

A sound geographic argument explains opportunities and obstacles. It never ranks the worth of peoples.

5Building a historical argument

PartWhat it doesExample
ThesisStates your claim in one sentence"Geography explains where civilisation began but not what form it took."

ReasoningLinks the facts to the claim"Because only such land produced a large surplus…"

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Use geography to explain one difference between Egypt and Mesopotamia, then give one limit of your explanation.

  1. Claim: Egypt's geography favoured unity and Mesopotamia's favoured division.
  2. Evidence: Egypt had one river, a natural highway, and deserts on both sides. Mesopotamia had two rivers, separate canal networks and open borders.
  3. Reasoning: a single protected valley is easy for one ruler to control and hard to invade, while exposed rival cities compete and are conquered in turn.
  4. Limit: Egypt did break apart at times, between the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms, although its geography had not changed. Something else, such as weak rulers or famine, must also matter.

Answer: Geography explains the tendency, but not every event.

Hands-on: The Great Debate

You need: notebook, the comparison table above, two or more people, a timer

  1. Motion for debate: "Geography decided the fate of the first civilisations."
  2. Prepare both sides. For each, list three pieces of evidence from Weeks 1 to 8.
  3. Toss a coin to decide which side you argue. Speak for two minutes. Your opponent replies for two minutes. Each of you then has one minute to answer the other's best point.
  4. Afterwards, drop your assigned side. Write your real opinion as a thesis sentence.
  5. Develop it into a paragraph with evidence, reasoning, a counter-argument and a conclusion.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What is geographic determinism?

    Answer and why

    The view that the physical environment decides how societies develop.

    WhyIt holds that climate, land and resources are the main causes of historical differences.

  2. What is possibilism?

    Answer and why

    The view that geography offers possibilities and limits, and people choose among them.

    WhyIt gives human culture, ideas and technology a real share in the outcome.

  3. Give one piece of evidence from Quarter 1 that supports determinism.

    Answer and why

    All three early civilisations arose on fertile floodplains in dry regions.

    WhyOnly there could irrigation farming produce the surplus that cities need.

  4. Give one piece of evidence that weakens determinism.

    Answer and why

    Egypt and the Indus Valley had similar river settings but built very different societies.

    WhyIf geography alone decided, similar environments should produce similar results.

  5. Why did the east–west shape of Eurasia help crops and ideas to spread, according to Diamond?

    Answer and why

    Places at the same latitude share similar climates and day lengths.

    WhyA crop bred in one place could grow thousands of kilometres east or west, but not far north or south.

  6. Why must geographic explanations be used carefully?

    Answer and why

    They have been misused to claim that some peoples are superior to others.

    WhyGeography explains opportunities and obstacles. It says nothing about the worth or ability of the people.

★ Scholar's challenge

Modern Singapore has almost no farmland, fresh water or natural resources, yet it is among the richest countries in the world. Japan lacks oil and iron and became an industrial power. What do these cases suggest about determinism today, and what has changed since ancient times?

Answer and why

Geography binds modern societies far less tightly, because trade, technology and education let people overcome local shortages.

WhyShips, desalination and global trade bring in what the land lacks, and wealth now comes largely from skills and ideas. A strong answer also notes that geography still matters: Singapore's position on a major sea route is itself a geographic advantage that its people chose to use.

Words to own

Determinism
The belief that outcomes are fixed by prior causes.
Possibilism
The view that environment limits but does not decide human choices.
Thesis
The main claim of an argument.
Counter-argument
An objection to your own claim, which you must answer.
Evidence
Facts that support a claim.
Diffusion
The spread of crops, inventions or ideas from one place to another.
