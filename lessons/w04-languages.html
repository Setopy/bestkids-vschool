Latin & Greek Number Roots I

The big questionA unicycle has one wheel, a bicycle two and a tricycle three. A monologue has one speaker, and a pentagon has five sides. Why does English have two complete sets of counting words hidden inside it?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Recall the Latin and Greek roots for the numbers one to five.
  2. Use those roots to decode unfamiliar words.
  3. Explain why English uses both Latin and Greek roots.
  4. Avoid a "false friend": a look-alike that is not a number root.

1Two toolkits

For two thousand years the languages of learning in Europe were Latin, the language of Rome, and Greek, the language of the first great scientists and philosophers. When scholars needed a new word they built it from these, and they still do.

As a rough guide, Latin roots are common in everyday and legal words, and Greek roots are common in science, mathematics and medicine. Knowing both sets lets you read thousands of words on sight.

5Beware the false friend

Not every "di" or "bi" means two. In dialogue, the prefix is dia-, meaning "through" or "across". A dialogue is speech passing between people, and it can have more than two speakers. In biology, bio- means life. Always test your guess against the meaning of the whole word.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Decode "quadrilateral" and "tetrapod".

  1. quadri- is Latin for four, and -lateral means side. Four sides.
  2. tetra- is Greek for four, and -pod means foot. Four feet.

Answer: A quadrilateral is a four-sided shape. A tetrapod is a four-footed animal.

Hands-on: Number-Root Hunt

You need: a dictionary, a timer, two colours of pen, paper

  1. Rule a page into five rows, for 1 to 5, and two columns: Latin and Greek.
  2. Set a timer for 15 minutes. Fill every box with as many real words as you can find.
  3. For each word, underline the number root in one colour and the partner root in the other.
  4. Invent five new creatures or machines with names built from these roots, such as a "pentaped". Draw one.
  5. Quiz a parent: say the definition and let them build the word.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What does "bilingual" mean, root by root?

    Answer and why

    bi- (two) + lingual (tongue or language): able to use two languages.

    WhyLatin lingua means tongue, which also gives "language" and "linguist".

  2. Which shape is a pentagon?

    Answer and why

    A shape with five angles, and so five sides.

    WhyGreek penta- is five and -gon is angle or corner.

  3. A "monopoly" is when one company controls all sales of something. Which root tells you that?

    Answer and why

    mono-, the Greek root for one

    Whymono- (one) joined to a root meaning "sell" gives "one seller".

  4. What is a quadruped? Name one.

    Answer and why

    An animal with four feet, such as a horse.

    WhyLatin quadr- is four and ped- is foot.

  5. Is the "di" in "dialogue" a number root?

    Answer and why

    No. It is dia-, meaning through or across.

    WhyA dialogue is speech between people, however many there are. Meaning must confirm a guess from spelling.

  6. How many books are in a trilogy? How many events are in a pentathlon?

    Answer and why

    Three books, five events

    Whytri- is three and penta- is five in Greek.

★ Scholar's challenge

A "unilateral" decision is made by one side alone. Work out what "bilateral", "trilateral" and "multilateral" talks would be, and build a sensible word for a shape with four angles using Greek roots.

Answer and why

Talks between two sides, three sides and many sides. The shape is a "tetragon".

Why-lateral means side, and the number root counts the sides taking part. tetra- (four) plus -gon (angle) is a real word, though "quadrilateral" is more common.

Words to own

The core part of a word that carries its main meaning.
Prefix
A word part placed before a root.
Latin
The language of ancient Rome, mother of the Romance languages.
Greek
The language of ancient Greece, source of much scientific vocabulary.
False friend
A word or word part that looks like another but has a different meaning.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Why would scientists keep building new words from two languages that nobody speaks at home any more?
  • Would science be easier to learn if every language made its own science words from its own roots?
  • English has "four-sided", "quadrilateral" and "tetragon" for one idea. Is having three words a strength or a waste?
