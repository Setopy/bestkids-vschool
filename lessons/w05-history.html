🏛 History & Geography · Week 5

The Agricultural Revolution

The big questionFor nearly all of human history every person was a hunter or a gatherer. Then, in several far-apart places within a few thousand years, people began to plant seeds. It changed everything. Was it the best decision humans ever made, or a trap?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Describe the hunter-gatherer way of life.
  2. Explain what domestication is and where the first farming began.
  3. Trace the chain from farming to surplus to cities.
  4. Weigh the gains and costs of the change.

1Life before farming

For hundreds of thousands of years people lived as hunter-gatherers. They moved with the seasons in bands of a few dozen, following animals and ripening plants. They owned only what they could carry. They knew hundreds of plants and the habits of every animal around them. This long period is the Palaeolithic, or Old Stone Age.

2The great change

About 12,000 years ago the last Ice Age ended and the climate became warmer and steadier. In a few regions people began to domesticate plants and animals: to breed them, generation after generation, for the features humans wanted. This was the Neolithic Revolution, the start of the New Stone Age.

It was not one event. It happened independently in several parts of the world.

The Fertile Crescent is an arc of well-watered land from the eastern Mediterranean to the Persian Gulf. In Mexico, farmers slowly turned a wild grass called teosinte, with ears the size of a finger, into maize. The dog had been domesticated from the wolf much earlier, by hunters.

3The chain reaction

Farming set off a sequence of changes, each causing the next.

  • Settlement. Fields cannot be carried, so people stayed in one place and built permanent villages.
  • Surplus. A good harvest gave more food than the farmers needed.
  • Population growth. More food fed more children.
  • Specialisation. Not everyone had to farm. Some became potters, weavers, toolmakers, priests and soldiers.
  • Property and trade. Stored grain and land could be owned, counted, exchanged and stolen.
  • Government and classes. Someone had to organise storage, defence and irrigation. Rulers and ranks appeared.

Early farming towns such as Jericho, near the River Jordan, and Çatalhöyük, in what is now Turkey, held thousands of people by 7000 BC.

4Gains and costs

GainsCosts
A steadier, storable food supplyHarder, longer work
Far larger populationsA narrower diet and poorer teeth and bones
Time for crafts, learning and inventionNew diseases caught from crowded living and animals
Villages, then citiesInequality, and war over land and stores

Skeletons show that early farmers were often shorter and less healthy than the hunter-gatherers before them. Yet farming peoples multiplied, and once a population had grown it could not go back. Every city, book and laboratory since then has depended on the farmer's surplus.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Explain how farming led to the invention of writing. Use at least three links of the chain.

  1. Farming produced a surplus of grain.
  2. Surplus had to be stored and shared out, so someone had to keep records.
  3. Surplus also fed specialists who did not farm, including full-time record-keepers.
  4. Marks for counting sacks and animals grew, over centuries, into written signs.

Answer: Farming, then surplus, then storage and specialists, then records, then writing. You will see this happen in Mesopotamia next week.

Hands-on: Forager or Farmer?

You need: paper, pencils, a few seeds (beans or maize) and a pot of soil

  1. Draw two columns: a day in the life of a 10-year-old forager and of a 10-year-old early farmer. Fill each with at least six activities.
  2. For each life list what you own, what you eat, and what you fear.
  3. Plant the seeds. Each day for two weeks, record what care they needed. How does a crop tie you to one place?
  4. Hold a family vote: which life would each person choose? Each voter must give one reason.
  5. Write a paragraph that begins: "The Agricultural Revolution was mostly a gain (or loss) because…"

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What does it mean to domesticate a plant or animal?

    Answer and why

    To breed it over many generations for traits that are useful to humans.

    WhyBy choosing which seeds to plant or which animals to keep, people slowly changed wild species into crops and livestock.

  2. Where is the Fertile Crescent and which crops began there?

    Answer and why

    In Southwest Asia, from the eastern Mediterranean to the Persian Gulf. Wheat and barley.

    WhyIts wild grasses, rivers and rainfall made it one of the first places where farming began.

  3. Which three crops were domesticated in Mesoamerica?

    Answer and why

    Maize, beans and squash

    WhyThese "three sisters" were developed in ancient Mexico, with maize bred from the wild grass teosinte.

  4. What is a surplus, and why does it matter?

    Answer and why

    More food than the producers need. It frees some people to do other work.

    WhySpecialists, rulers, trade and cities are all fed from the surplus.

  5. Give two ways in which early farmers were worse off than hunter-gatherers.

    Answer and why

    They worked harder, ate a narrower diet and caught more diseases (any two).

    WhyEvidence from skeletons shows shorter height and more illness after the change to farming.

  6. Why did farming begin when it did and not 50,000 years earlier?

    Answer and why

    The climate became warmer and more stable after the last Ice Age.

    WhyCrops need dependable seasons. Before about 12,000 years ago the climate was too cold and changeable.

★ Scholar's challenge

Farming began separately in at least five regions that had no contact with each other. What does that suggest about why it happened? Give two possible explanations.

Answer and why

It suggests shared causes, not one clever inventor: for example the worldwide change in climate, and growing populations that needed more food.

WhyWhen the same change appears independently in many places, the explanation must lie in conditions common to all of them. A strong answer also notes that each region used the plants and animals that happened to live there.

Words to own

Hunter-gatherer
A person who lives by hunting animals and collecting wild plants.
Neolithic
The New Stone Age, when farming began.
Domestication
Changing wild plants or animals by selective breeding.
Surplus
An amount beyond what is needed.
Specialisation
Different people doing different kinds of work.
Sedentary
Living in one place; not moving with the seasons.
