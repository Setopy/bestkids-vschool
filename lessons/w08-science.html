⚛ Science · Week 8

The pH Scale

The big questionLemon juice and stomach acid are both acids, but you can drink one and the other would burn your skin. Soap and oven cleaner are both bases, but only one is safe on your hands. How do scientists measure how strong an acid or a base is?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Describe the properties of acids and bases.
  2. Read the pH scale and place common substances on it.
  3. Use indicators to test whether a substance is acidic, neutral or basic.
  4. Explain neutralisation and give everyday uses for it.

1Acids and bases

Many substances that dissolve in water fall into two opposite families.

AcidsBases (alkalis when dissolved)
TasteSourBitter
FeelSharp, stingingSlippery, soapy
In water they releaseHydrogen ions, H⁺Hydroxide ions, OH⁻
ExamplesLemon juice, vinegar, stomach acidBaking soda, soap, bleach

Never taste or touch a chemical to test it. Strong acids and strong bases are both corrosive: they destroy skin and other materials. Taste and feel are described only so that you can recognise the families in foods and soaps you already know.

2The pH scale

In 1909 the Danish chemist Søren Sørensen introduced the pH scale, which measures how acidic or basic a solution is. It runs from 0 to 14.

pHSubstanceClass
1 to 2Stomach acidStrong acid
2Lemon juiceAcid
3VinegarAcid
4Tomato juiceAcid
5.6Clean rainWeak acid
6.5MilkVery weak acid
7Pure waterNeutral
7.4BloodVery weak base
8Sea waterWeak base
9Baking soda solutionBase
10Soapy waterBase
12 to 13BleachStrong base
14Drain cleanerVery strong base

Below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, and above 7 is basic. The further from 7, the stronger.

The scale has a hidden power. Each step of 1 is a tenfold change. A solution of pH 3 is ten times more acidic than one of pH 4 and a hundred times more acidic than one of pH 5. This is why stomach acid at pH 1 is so much fiercer than tomato juice at pH 4: it is 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000 times more acidic.

3Indicators

An indicator is a substance that changes colour according to pH.

IndicatorIn acidNeutralIn base
LitmusRedPurpleBlue
Universal indicatorRed, orange, yellowGreenBlue, purple
Red cabbage juiceRed, pinkPurpleGreen, yellow

Litmus tells you only acid or base. Universal indicator shows a range of colours, so it tells you the approximate pH.

4Neutralisation

When an acid and a base are mixed in the right amounts they cancel each other. This reaction is neutralisation:

acid + base → salt + water

The hydrogen ions and hydroxide ions join to make water, H₂O. The remaining ions form a salt. Table salt is one example of a salt, but there are many others.

ProblemNeutralised with
Too much stomach acid (indigestion)An antacid tablet, a mild base
Soil too acidic for cropsLime, a base
Acids made by bacteria on teethToothpaste, mildly basic
Acidic waste from a factoryA base, before the water is released

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

A solution has pH 2 and another has pH 5. Which is more acidic, and by how many times?

  1. A lower pH means more acidic, so the pH 2 solution is more acidic.
  2. The difference is 3 steps.
  3. Each step is a factor of 10: 10 × 10 × 10.

Answer: The pH 2 solution is 1,000 times more acidic.

Hands-on: Red Cabbage Rainbow

You need: a quarter of a red cabbage, hot water, a sieve, six clear glasses, a dropper or spoon, lemon juice, white vinegar, tap water, baking soda, soapy water, a clear fizzy drink. An adult handles the hot water.

  1. Chop the cabbage, cover it with hot water and leave for 15 minutes. Strain the purple liquid. This is your indicator.
  2. Put a little of each test liquid in its own glass. Predict whether each is an acid or a base.
  3. Add a spoonful of indicator to each. Record the colours and arrange the glasses from most acidic to most basic.
  4. Take the vinegar glass. Add baking soda solution one spoonful at a time, stirring. Watch the colour travel back toward purple. What reaction is taking place?
  5. Draw your own colour chart, with an estimated pH for each liquid.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What pH is neutral? Give an example.

    Answer and why

    pH 7, pure water

    WhyAt pH 7 the hydrogen ions and hydroxide ions are in balance.

  2. A liquid turns litmus paper blue. Is it an acid or a base?

    Answer and why

    A base

    WhyLitmus is red in acids and blue in bases.

  3. Which is more acidic, vinegar at pH 3 or milk at pH 6.5?

    Answer and why

    Vinegar

    WhyThe lower the pH, the more acidic the solution.

  4. How many times more acidic is pH 4 than pH 6?

    Answer and why

    100 times

    WhyTwo steps of the scale, each a factor of ten: 10 × 10.

  5. Complete the word equation: acid + base → ___ + ___.

    Answer and why

    salt + water

    WhyThe acid's hydrogen ions join the base's hydroxide ions to make water, and the remaining ions form a salt.

  6. Why does an antacid tablet relieve indigestion?

    Answer and why

    It is a mild base that neutralises excess stomach acid.

    WhyNeutralisation raises the pH of the stomach contents toward neutral.

★ Scholar's challenge

A farmer tests her soil and finds pH 5. Her beans grow best at about pH 6.5. What should she add, what reaction will take place, and why must she add it gradually and test again?

Answer and why

She should add a base such as lime. It neutralises some of the acid, raising the pH. Too much would make the soil basic.

WhyAcid plus base gives salt and water, moving the pH toward 7. Because each pH step is a tenfold change, small additions have large effects, and plants are harmed by soil that is too basic as well as too acidic.

Words to own

Acid
A substance that releases hydrogen ions in water; pH below 7.
Base
A substance that neutralises acids; pH above 7.
pH scale
A scale from 0 to 14 measuring acidity.
Indicator
A substance that changes colour with pH.
Neutralisation
The reaction of an acid with a base to give a salt and water.
Corrosive
Able to destroy skin, metal or other materials chemically.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Why is a tenfold scale more useful here than an ordinary one? Where else might such a scale be used?
  • Burning coal and oil can make rain more acidic. Who should be responsible for the lakes and forests it damages?
