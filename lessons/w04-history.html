🏛 History & Geography · Week 4

Hydrology

The big questionEarth is called the blue planet, yet if all its water filled a 100-litre barrel, the fresh water people can easily reach would be less than a teaspoon. Where is all the water, and why did every early civilisation grow beside a river?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Describe how Earth's water is divided between oceans, ice, groundwater and surface water.
  2. Label the parts of a river system from source to mouth.
  3. Explain what a watershed and an aquifer are.
  4. Explain why rivers were essential to early civilisations.

1Where the water is

Hydrology is the study of water on Earth: where it is, how it moves, and how people use it.

StoreShare of all water
Oceans (salt water)about 97%
Fresh waterabout 3%

Of that small share of fresh water:

Store of fresh waterShare
Ice caps and glaciersabout 69%
Groundwaterabout 30%
Lakes, rivers, swamps and airabout 1%

Almost all the water people drink, wash with and farm with comes from that last sliver, together with groundwater from wells.

2The anatomy of a river

PartMeaning
SourceWhere a river begins: a spring, lake, or melting snow
TributaryA smaller river that flows into a larger one
ConfluenceThe point where two rivers join
MeanderA wide bend in a river on flat land
FloodplainFlat land beside a river, covered when it floods
MouthWhere a river enters the sea or a lake
DeltaLand built from mud dropped at the mouth

A river with all its tributaries is a river system. The whole area of land that drains into it is its watershed, or drainage basin. Rain falling on one side of a ridge runs to one river, and rain on the other side runs to another. That ridge is a divide.

Rivers always flow downhill, from high ground to low. They do not always flow south. The Nile flows north.

3Water underground

Rain soaks down until it fills the gaps in rock and soil. This is groundwater. A layer of rock that holds usable water is an aquifer, and the top of the soaked zone is the water table. A well is a hole dug below the water table. If people pump water out faster than rain refills it, the water table falls and wells run dry.

4Rivers and the first civilisations

RiverCivilisation
Tigris and EuphratesMesopotamia
NileEgypt
IndusIndus Valley
Huang He (Yellow River)Ancient China

A river gave drinking water, fish, and a road for boats. Above all, its floods spread fresh silt, fine fertile mud, over the fields each year. With irrigation canals, farmers could grow more food than they needed. That surplus let some people become potters, priests, soldiers and scribes, and cities were born.

Rivers still shape politics. The Colorado River is used so heavily for farms and cities that in most years it no longer reaches the sea at its delta in Mexico.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Rain falls on a mountain ridge. On the west side streams run to River A, and on the east side to River B. What is the ridge called, and what are the two areas?

  1. Land draining to one river is that river's watershed.
  2. The high ground separating two watersheds is a divide.

Answer: The ridge is a divide. West of it is River A's watershed and east of it is River B's.

Hands-on: Crumpled-Paper Watershed

You need: a sheet of stiff paper, washable markers, a spray bottle of water, a tray

  1. Crumple the paper, then open it out partly so it has peaks and valleys. Set it on the tray.
  2. With a blue marker, draw where you predict rivers will run. With brown, trace the highest ridges.
  3. Spray "rain" gently from above and watch where the colour flows and pools.
  4. Mark the divides, one watershed and one lake. Were your predictions right?
  5. Add a red dot for a "factory" high up. Spray again. Where does its pollution travel? Who lives downstream?

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. About what share of Earth's water is fresh?

    Answer and why

    About 3%

    WhyRoughly 97% is salt water in the oceans.

  2. Where is most of the world's fresh water stored?

    Answer and why

    In ice caps and glaciers

    WhyAbout 69% of fresh water is frozen, mainly in Antarctica and Greenland.

  3. What is a tributary?

    Answer and why

    A smaller river or stream that flows into a larger river.

    WhyTributaries gather water from across the watershed and deliver it to the main river.

  4. Why does a river drop mud to build a delta at its mouth?

    Answer and why

    The water slows down when it meets the sea, so it can no longer carry its sediment.

    WhyFast water carries mud and sand. When the current slows, the particles settle.

  5. Explain why the yearly flood was a gift to ancient farmers.

    Answer and why

    It spread fertile silt over the fields and soaked the soil.

    WhyThe fresh mud renewed the soil's nutrients, so the same land could be farmed year after year.

  6. A town pumps water from its aquifer faster than rain refills it. What will happen?

    Answer and why

    The water table will fall and wells will run dry.

    WhyAn aquifer is a store. Taking out more than goes in empties it over time.

★ Scholar's challenge

A river flows through three countries before reaching the sea. The first country builds a large dam to irrigate its farms. Describe one benefit to that country and two problems for the countries downstream. Who should decide how the water is shared?

Answer and why

Benefit: a reliable supply for farms, and often electricity. Problems: less water downstream, and less silt reaching the fields and the delta.

WhyWater used or held upstream cannot flow on, and a dam traps sediment. There is no single right answer to the last question, but a strong one explains a fair rule, such as sharing by need, by population or by treaty.

Words to own

Bringing water to crops by canals, ditches or pipes.
