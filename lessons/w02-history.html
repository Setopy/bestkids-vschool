🏛 History & Geography · Week 2

Topography

The big questionMexico City stands 2,240 metres above the sea, ringed by volcanoes. Why would millions of people choose to live so high, and how does the shape of the land decide where cities grow?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Name and describe the major landforms.
  2. Read elevation from contour lines on a topographic map.
  3. Explain how mountains create a rain shadow.
  4. Explain how landforms shape where and how people live.

1The shape of the land

Topography is the study of the shape of the Earth's surface. Elevation is height above sea level. Relief is the difference between the highest and lowest points of an area.

LandformWhat it isExample
MountainHigh, steep land rising well above its surroundingsHimalaya, Andes, Sierra Madre
PlateauHigh land with a mostly flat topTibetan Plateau, Mexican Plateau
PlainWide, low, flat landNorth China Plain, the Pampas
ValleyLow land between hills, often carved by a riverNile Valley
DeltaFan of mud dropped where a river meets the seaNile Delta, Mekong Delta
PeninsulaLand surrounded by water on three sidesBaja California, Yucatán
IsthmusNarrow strip joining two larger landsIsthmus of Panama

The highest point on Earth is the summit of Mount Everest at 8,849 m. The lowest dry land is the shore of the Dead Sea, more than 400 m below sea level.

2Reading contour lines

A topographic map shows height with contour lines. Every point on one line is at the same elevation. The contour interval is the height difference between neighbouring lines.

  • Lines close together mean a steep slope.
  • Lines far apart mean a gentle slope.
  • Closed rings, one inside another, show a hill. The smallest ring is the top.
  • Contour lines never cross.

3Mountains make their own weather

When wet wind from the sea meets a mountain range, the air is forced up. Rising air cools, its water vapour condenses, and rain falls on the windward side. The air that crosses the top is dry. The far, leeward side receives little rain. This dry zone is a rain shadow. The Atacama Desert behind the Andes and the deserts of northern Mexico are partly rain-shadow deserts.

4Land shapes history

People settle where the land helps them: flat plains for farming, river valleys for water and transport, natural harbours for trade. Mountains protect and isolate. Egypt was guarded by deserts. Greece, cut up by mountains, grew into many small independent cities. In the tropics, highlands are cooler and healthier than the hot lowlands, which is one reason great cities such as Mexico City, Bogotá and Addis Ababa stand high.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

A map has a contour interval of 20 m. A hiker walks from a line marked 340 m, crossing four more contour lines uphill. What is her elevation?

  1. Each line crossed uphill adds one interval of 20 m.
  2. Four lines add 4 × 20 = 80 m.
  3. 340 + 80 = 420.

Answer: 420 m

Hands-on: Build a Contour Mountain

You need: modelling clay or salt dough, dental floss or thread, pencil, paper, ruler

  1. Shape a mountain about 8 cm tall with one steep side and one gentle side.
  2. Mark a vertical scale on a pencil every 2 cm and score matching rings round the mountain.
  3. Slice the mountain along each ring with the floss, from the bottom up.
  4. Trace the base of each slice onto paper, largest first, keeping them lined up.
  5. You have drawn a topographic map. Where are the lines closest together? Label the contour interval.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What is the difference between a plateau and a plain?

    Answer and why

    Both are flat, but a plateau is high and a plain is low.

    WhyLandforms are sorted by both shape and elevation. A plateau is sometimes called a tableland.

  2. Contour lines on one side of a hill are very close together. What does this tell a hiker?

    Answer and why

    That side is steep.

    WhyClose lines mean the height changes a lot over a short horizontal distance.

  3. Why can two contour lines never cross?

    Answer and why

    A single point cannot have two different elevations.

    WhyEach line stands for one height. A crossing would claim the same spot is at both heights at once.

  4. Which side of a mountain range is usually wetter, windward or leeward?

    Answer and why

    Windward

    WhyAir rising up the windward side cools and drops its rain there, leaving dry air to descend on the leeward side.

  5. The lowest point of a region is 150 m and the highest is 2,900 m. What is the relief?

    Answer and why

    2,750 m

    WhyRelief is the difference between highest and lowest: 2,900 − 150.

  6. Give two reasons why ancient people often built cities in river valleys.

    Answer and why

    Fresh water and fertile soil (also transport and flat land for building).

    WhyRivers supply drinking water, irrigation and rich mud for crops, and boats move heavy goods cheaply.

★ Scholar's challenge

Ancient Greece became many small city-states, while ancient Egypt became one long kingdom. Use topography to explain the difference.

Answer and why

Greece's mountains and islands separated communities. The Nile linked Egypt into one strip.

WhyMountains make travel hard, so each Greek valley governed itself. The Nile was an easy highway through flat land, so one ruler could reach and control the whole valley.

Words to own

Topography
The shape of the land surface, and the study of it.
Elevation
Height above sea level.
Relief
The difference between the highest and lowest points in an area.
Contour line
A map line joining points of equal elevation.
Rain shadow
A dry region on the sheltered side of mountains.
Delta
Land built from sediment where a river enters the sea or a lake.
