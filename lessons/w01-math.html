∑ Mathematics · Week 1

Non-Decimal Bases

The big questionWhen you count 1, 2, 3 … up to 9, the next number suddenly needs two digits. Why does that happen at 9 and not at 7 or 15? Who decided?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Explain what a base is and why base ten is only one choice among many.
  2. Convert a number from base ten into base two, five or twelve, and back again.
  3. Spot other bases hiding in daily life: clocks, egg cartons, computers.
  4. Check every conversion by working backwards.

1What a base really is

A base is the size of the bundle. It tells you how many objects you must collect before you trade them for one object in the next column. In base ten we bundle at ten: ten ones become one ten, ten tens become one hundred.

We almost certainly use ten because humans have ten fingers. That is an accident of biology, not a law of mathematics. A creature with eight fingers would write "10" to mean one bundle of eight and nothing left over.

The value of a digit depends on where it sits. This is place value. In base ten the columns are worth 1, 10, 100, 1000. Each column is ten times the one on its right.

BaseBundle sizeColumn values (right to left)Digits allowed
Two (binary)21, 2, 4, 8, 16, 320, 1
Five51, 5, 25, 125, 6250, 1, 2, 3, 4
Ten (decimal)101, 10, 100, 10000 to 9
Twelve (dozenal)121, 12, 144, 17280 to 9, then A = ten, B = eleven

Notice the pattern: a base never has a digit equal to itself. Base five has no digit 5, just as base ten has no single digit for ten.

2Where other bases hide

  • Base two runs every computer. A switch is either off (0) or on (1), so a machine made of switches must count in twos.
  • Base twelve survives in 12 hours on a clock face, 12 months, and eggs by the dozen. Twelve divides evenly by 2, 3, 4 and 6. Ten divides evenly only by 2 and 5.
  • Base sixty came from ancient Mesopotamia and still gives us 60 seconds in a minute and 60 minutes in an hour.

We write the base as a small number after the numeral: 111₂ means "one-one-one in base two". With no small number, assume base ten.

3The two methods

From base ten to another base: repeated division. Divide by the new base, write the remainder, and repeat with the quotient until the quotient is 0. Read the remainders from the last to the first.

From another base to base ten: expand by place value. Multiply each digit by the value of its column and add.

A scholar never trusts a conversion until it has been checked in the opposite direction.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Convert 13 to base two.

  1. 13 ÷ 2 = 6 remainder 1
  2. 6 ÷ 2 = 3 remainder 0
  3. 3 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 1
  4. 1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1
  5. Read the remainders from last to first: 1, 1, 0, 1.
  6. Check: 1×8 + 1×4 + 0×2 + 1×1 = 13. ✓

Answer: 13 = 1101₂

Example 2

Convert 2301₅ to base ten.

  1. The columns in base five are worth 125, 25, 5 and 1.
  2. 2×125 = 250, 3×25 = 75, 0×5 = 0, 1×1 = 1.
  3. Add: 250 + 75 + 0 + 1 = 326.

Answer: 2301₅ = 326

Example 3

Convert 100 to base twelve.

  1. 100 ÷ 12 = 8 remainder 4
  2. 8 ÷ 12 = 0 remainder 8
  3. Read from last to first: 8, 4.
  4. Check: 8×12 + 4 = 100. ✓

Answer: 100 = 84₁₂

Hands-on: The Five-Egg Farm

You need: 60 dried beans or coins, small cups, a sheet of paper ruled into three columns labelled 25s, 5s and 1s

  1. Count out 47 beans. The farm rule: you may never leave five or more loose beans.
  2. Bundle the beans into cups of five. Count the cups and the loose beans.
  3. Now you may never leave five or more loose cups. Stack five cups into a "crate" of 25.
  4. Record crates, cups and loose beans in the three columns. You have written 47 in base five.
  5. Repeat with 38 and 112. Then invent a Three-Egg Farm and write 20 in base three.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Convert 47 to base five.

    Answer and why

    142₅

    Why47 ÷ 5 = 9 r 2, 9 ÷ 5 = 1 r 4, 1 ÷ 5 = 0 r 1. Reading last to first gives 1, 4, 2. Check: 25 + 20 + 2 = 47.

  2. Convert 10110₂ to base ten.

    Answer and why

    22

    WhyThe columns are 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. The 1s sit in the 16, 4 and 2 columns, and 16 + 4 + 2 = 22.

  3. Convert 59 to base two.

    Answer and why

    111011₂

    Why59 = 32 + 16 + 8 + 2 + 1. Put a 1 in each of those columns and a 0 in the 4 column.

  4. What is 3A₁₂ in base ten? (A stands for ten.)

    Answer and why

    46

    Why3 twelves is 36, plus ten ones gives 46.

  5. Why is 728₅ not a real base-five number?

    Answer and why

    Because the digits 7 and 8 do not exist in base five.

    WhyIn base five you bundle as soon as you reach five, so the only digits you can ever have left over are 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4.

  6. Write 144 in base twelve.

    Answer and why

    100₁₂

    Why144 is exactly one bundle of twelve twelves. That fills the third column with 1 and leaves nothing in the other two.

  7. Add in binary without converting: 1011₂ + 110₂.

    Answer and why

    10001₂

    WhyWork right to left and carry whenever a column reaches two. Check in base ten: 11 + 6 = 17, and 17 = 16 + 1 = 10001₂.

★ Scholar's challenge

A number is written 121 in some unknown base, and it equals 36 in base ten. What is the base?

Answer and why

Base five

WhyIn base b, 121 means b×b + 2×b + 1. Try b = 5: 25 + 10 + 1 = 36. (That expression is also (b + 1)×(b + 1), and 6×6 = 36, so b = 5.)

Words to own

Base
The bundle size of a number system; how many of one column you trade for one of the next.
Place value
The value a digit has because of the column it sits in.
Binary
Base two, using only the digits 0 and 1.
Remainder
What is left over after dividing into equal groups.
Digit
A single symbol used to write numbers.
