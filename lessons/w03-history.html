🏛 History & Geography · Week 3

Biomes

The big questionA polar bear and a camel are both mammals, yet swap their homes and neither would last a week. What sorts the living world into such different zones?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Define a biome and explain what climate is.
  2. Describe the main land biomes by temperature, rainfall, plants and animals.
  3. Explain how latitude and altitude control where biomes occur.
  4. Explain how people have adapted to different biomes.

1Climate decides

Weather is what the air is doing today. Climate is the pattern of weather over thirty years or more. A biome is a very large region with a similar climate and therefore similar plants and animals.

Two measurements matter most: temperature and precipitation (rain and snow). Plants respond to these, animals depend on the plants, and people depend on both.

2The major land biomes

BiomeClimateTypical life
TundraVery cold, dry, frozen subsoilMosses, lichens, caribou, arctic fox
Taiga (boreal forest)Long cold winters, short summersConifers, moose, wolves
Temperate forestFour seasons, steady rainOak, maple, deer, bears
GrasslandWarm summers, too dry for forestGrasses, bison, zebra, lions
DesertUnder 250 mm of rain a yearCacti, succulents, reptiles, camels
Mediterranean scrubHot dry summers, mild wet wintersOlive, shrubs, goats
Tropical rainforestHot all year, over 2,000 mm of rainThe greatest variety of life on land

Grasslands have many names: savanna in Africa, steppe in Asia, prairie in North America, pampas in South America. A desert is defined by dryness, not heat. Antarctica is the largest desert on Earth.

3Why the biomes lie in bands

The Sun's rays strike the equator directly and the poles at a slant, so it is hot near 0° and cold near 90°. Biomes therefore run in broad bands by latitude: rainforest at the equator, deserts near 30°, forests in the middle latitudes, tundra near the poles.

Altitude copies latitude. Temperature drops about 6.5 °C for every 1,000 m you climb. Walking up a tall tropical mountain is like travelling from the equator toward the pole: rainforest, then cloud forest, then grassland, then ice.

4People and biomes

BiomeA human adaptation
DesertOases, loose robes, travel by night, camels
TundraFur clothing, hunting and herding in place of farming
GrasslandHerding on horseback; later, wheat and maize farming
RainforestSmall shifting gardens, houses on stilts
River valleys in dry landsIrrigation and the first cities

The grasslands and river valleys became the world's breadbaskets. This is why the first civilisations, which you will meet from Week 5, rose where they did.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

A place has an average temperature of 27 °C in every month and receives 2,600 mm of rain a year. Name its biome and give your reasons.

  1. The temperature is high and hardly changes, so it lies near the equator.
  2. 2,600 mm is above the 2,000 mm mark.

Answer: Tropical rainforest: hot all year and very wet.

Hands-on: Shoebox Biome and Climograph

You need: a shoebox, natural and craft materials, graph paper, the monthly temperature and rainfall of one city (from an atlas or online)

  1. Choose one biome. Research three plants and three animals that live there.
  2. Build the biome inside the shoebox. Label one adaptation for each living thing.
  3. Draw a climograph for a city in that biome: months along the bottom, rainfall as bars, temperature as a line.
  4. On the back of the box, write how people traditionally built homes, dressed and found food there.
  5. Present it in two minutes without notes.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What is the difference between weather and climate?

    Answer and why

    Weather is the day-to-day state of the air. Climate is the average pattern over decades.

    WhyOne cold day is weather. A place being cold every winter for thirty years is climate.

  2. Which two measurements best predict a biome?

    Answer and why

    Temperature and precipitation

    WhyTogether they decide which plants can grow, and the plants decide the rest of the food web.

  3. Why is Antarctica classed as a desert?

    Answer and why

    It receives very little precipitation.

    WhyA desert is defined by dryness, under about 250 mm a year, not by heat.

  4. A mountain on the equator has snow at its summit. Explain.

    Answer and why

    Temperature falls with altitude, by about 6.5 °C per 1,000 m.

    WhyAt 5,000 m the air is more than 30 °C colder than at sea level, cold enough for permanent ice.

  5. Give the local name for grassland in Africa and in North America.

    Answer and why

    Savanna and prairie

    WhyThe same biome occurs on several continents, and each language named its own.

  6. Why did herding, and not crop farming, develop first on the dry steppe?

    Answer and why

    There is enough rain for grass but not for most crops.

    WhyAnimals can turn grass, which people cannot eat, into milk and meat, and herds can move to follow the rain.

★ Scholar's challenge

Baja California and Florida lie at similar latitudes, yet one is mostly desert and the other is green and humid. Suggest why latitude alone does not decide the biome.

Answer and why

Ocean currents, wind direction and mountains also control rainfall.

WhyA cold current runs along Baja's Pacific coast and cool air holds little moisture, while mountains block rain from the east. Florida is bathed by warm, wet air from the Gulf of Mexico and the Atlantic.

Words to own

Biome
A large region with similar climate, plants and animals.
Climate
The usual pattern of weather over many years.
Precipitation
Water that falls from the sky as rain, snow or hail.
Adaptation
A feature or behaviour that helps a living thing survive in its environment.
Altitude
Height above sea level.
Climograph
A chart showing a place's monthly temperature and rainfall.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Which biome would be hardest for humans to live in without modern technology? Defend your choice.
  • When people cut down a rainforest to plant crops, have they changed the biome or only the plants?
  • If the climate warms, what should happen to the bands of biomes? Who would gain and who would lose?
