Bar Modeling II: Multi-Step Problems

The big questionTwo friends start with the same amount of money. One spends $45 and the other spends $120. Now the first has four times as much as the second. With no algebra at all, can you find what they started with?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Model before-and-after problems with two sets of bars.
  2. Model "a fraction of the remainder" problems.
  3. Use what stays constant (a total or a difference) to find one unit.
  4. Solve transfer problems where something moves from one person to another.

1Find what does not change

Multi-step problems describe a change. The secret is to ask: what stays the same?

SituationWhat stays constant
Both people spend or gain different amountsNothing obvious: compare the bars after
Both people ageThe difference in their ages
One person gives to the otherThe total
Only one quantity changesThe other quantity

Draw a before model and an after model, one above the other, and mark the constant.

2Fraction of the remainder

When a problem says "then spent half of the remainder", the second fraction is taken from what is left, not from the original amount. Draw the first cut, then cut only the leftover part.

Here one third is spent first. The remainder is two thirds, and half of it is one of the original thirds. So all three pieces are equal, and each is $24.

3Transfers

When Kim gives stickers to Pat, Kim's bar shrinks and Pat's grows by the same amount. The total does not change. To make two unequal amounts equal, you move half of the difference.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Ana and Ben had the same amount of money. Ana spent $45 and Ben spent $120. Then Ana had 4 times as much as Ben. How much did each have at first?

  1. After: Ben is 1 unit and Ana is 4 units.
  2. Ben spent 120 − 45 = $75 more than Ana, so Ana now has $75 more. That difference is 3 units.
  3. 3 units = 75, so 1 unit = 25. Ben now has $25.
  4. Ben at first: 25 + 120 = 145.
  5. Check with Ana: 4 × 25 = 100, and 100 + 45 = 145. ✓

Answer: Each had $145 at first.

Example 2

Li spent 1/3 of her money on a book and 1/2 of the remainder on a game. She had $24 left. How much did she have at first?

  1. After the book, 2/3 remains.
  2. Half of 2/3 is 1/3, spent on the game. So 1/3 is left.
  3. 1/3 of her money = $24, so the whole is 3 × 24 = 72.
  4. Check: book 24, remainder 48, game 24, left 24. ✓

Answer: She had $72 at first.

Example 3

A father is 36 and his son is 8. In how many years will the father be 3 times as old as his son?

  1. The difference in their ages is always 36 − 8 = 28.
  2. When the father is 3 units and the son 1 unit, the difference is 2 units.
  3. 2 units = 28, so 1 unit = 14. The son will be 14.
  4. 14 − 8 = 6 years. Check: the father will be 42 = 3 × 14. ✓

Answer: In 6 years.

Hands-on: The Problem Workshop

You need: notebook, ruler, two coloured pencils

  1. Choose one worked example. Change only the numbers so that the answer is still a whole number. Solve your new problem with a full model.
  2. Write one transfer problem about two members of your family and something they collect.
  3. Draw the before model in one colour and the after model in another.
  4. Ask a parent to solve it using only your drawing, without reading the words.
  5. If they cannot, improve the labels on the drawing, not the words.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. A baker made 240 rolls. He sold 3/8 of them in the morning and 1/3 of the remainder in the afternoon. How many were left?

    Answer and why

    100 rolls

    WhyMorning: 3/8 of 240 = 90, leaving 150. Afternoon: 1/3 of 150 = 50. Then 150 − 50 = 100.

  2. Kim has 80 stickers and Pat has 32. How many must Kim give Pat so that they have the same number?

    Answer and why

    24 stickers

    WhyThe total, 112, stays constant, so each ends with 56. Kim gives 80 − 56 = 24, which is half the difference of 48.

  3. Rosa had 5 times as many stamps as Dan. After Rosa gave Dan 30 stamps, they had the same number. How many did Rosa have at first?

    Answer and why

    75 stamps

    WhyThe difference was 4 units, and giving 30 closes a gap of 60. So 4 units = 60 and 1 unit = 15. Rosa had 5 × 15 = 75. Check: 75 − 30 = 45 and 15 + 30 = 45.

  4. In a class, 2/5 of the pupils are boys. There are 12 more girls than boys. How many pupils are in the class?

    Answer and why

    60 pupils

    WhyBoys are 2 units and girls 3 units. The difference, 1 unit, is 12. The class is 5 units = 60.

  5. Three pens and two notebooks cost $19. A notebook costs $2 more than a pen. What does a pen cost?

    Answer and why

    $3

    WhySwap each notebook for a pen plus $2: five pens plus $4 cost $19, so five pens cost $15. Check: 3 × 3 + 2 × 5 = 19.

  6. Mei spent 1/4 of her money on lunch and $18 on a book. She then had half of her money left. How much did she have at first?

    Answer and why

    $72

    WhyHalf was spent in all. Lunch was one quarter, so the book was the other quarter: 1/4 = $18, and the whole is $72.

★ Scholar's challenge

Alma and Bruno have $200 between them. After Alma gives Bruno $30, she still has $20 more than he does. How much did each have at first?

Answer and why

Alma $140, Bruno $60

WhyThe total stays $200. Afterwards, removing the extra 20 leaves two equal bars: 180 ÷ 2 = 90 for Bruno and 110 for Alma. Undo the gift: Alma 110 + 30 = 140, Bruno 90 − 30 = 60.

Words to own

Before-and-after model
Two bar models showing a situation at two moments.
Remainder
What is left after part has been taken away.
Constant
A quantity that does not change.
Transfer
Moving an amount from one quantity to another.
Unit
One of the equal pieces of a bar model.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Why does the difference between two people's ages never change, while the ratio does?
  • In a transfer, the gap closes by twice the amount moved. Explain why in your own words.
  • Would you sooner solve these problems with bars or with letters such as x? What does each method make easy?
