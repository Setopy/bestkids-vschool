🏛 History & Geography · Week 1

Cartography & Map Projections

The big questionOn most classroom maps Greenland looks as big as Africa. In truth Africa is about fourteen times larger. Why would a map lie?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Explain why every flat map of the round Earth must distort something.
  2. Locate places using latitude and longitude.
  3. Compare the Mercator, equal-area and compromise projections.
  4. Use a map scale to calculate real distances.

1The orange-peel problem

Cartography is the art and science of making maps. The Earth is a sphere (very nearly), and a map is flat. Peel an orange and try to press the peel flat on a table: it tears or stretches. The same thing happens to the surface of the Earth.

A projection is a method of transferring the globe onto a flat sheet. Every projection distorts at least one of four things: shape, area, distance or direction. A mapmaker must choose what to keep true and what to sacrifice. That choice depends on what the map is for.

2Three famous projections

ProjectionKeeps trueDistortsBest for
Mercator (1569)Compass direction and local shapeArea, badly, near the polesSea navigation
Equal-area (for example Gall-Peters)AreaShape, stretched tall near the equatorComparing sizes of countries
Compromise (Robinson, Winkel Tripel)Nothing perfectlyEverything a littleGeneral world maps

Gerardus Mercator designed his map for sailors. A straight line on it is a line of constant compass bearing, so a navigator could set one heading and follow it. The price is that lands far from the equator swell. Greenland, Canada and Russia look enormous, while Africa, India and Mexico look smaller than they are.

3The global address system

  • Latitude lines run east–west and measure how far north or south you are from the equator (0°), up to 90° at each pole.
  • Longitude lines run from pole to pole and measure how far east or west you are from the prime meridian (0°) through Greenwich, England, up to 180°.

Latitude is always given first. Mexico City is near 19° N, 99° W. Bangkok is near 14° N, 100° E.

4Scale

A scale tells you how much the real world was shrunk. A scale of 1 : 50,000 means 1 cm on the map is 50,000 cm on the ground, which is 500 m.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

A map has a scale of 1 : 200,000. Two towns are 7 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they really?

  1. 1 cm on the map is 200,000 cm on the ground.
  2. 200,000 cm = 2,000 m = 2 km.
  3. 7 cm on the map is 7 × 2 km.

Answer: 14 km

Example 2

Which city is farther north: one at 33° N, 117° W or one at 7° N, 100° E?

  1. Latitude is the first number and tells north–south position.
  2. 33° N is farther from the equator than 7° N.

Answer: The city at 33° N

Hands-on: Flatten the World

You need: an orange or a mandarin, a marker pen, a globe or online globe, a printed Mercator world map

  1. Draw the equator and rough continents on the orange with the marker.
  2. Peel it carefully in as few pieces as possible and press the peel flat. Sketch what happened to your continents.
  3. On the Mercator map, cut out a paper shape of Greenland and lay it over Africa. Then compare the two on the globe.
  4. Find the latitude and longitude of your home, and of three cities you would like to visit.
  5. Write three sentences: what did the map get wrong, and why did the mapmaker accept that?

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Name the four properties a map projection can distort.

    Answer and why

    Shape, area, distance and direction

    WhyA curved surface cannot be flattened without stretching or tearing, so at least one of these must give way.

  2. Why did sailors prefer the Mercator projection?

    Answer and why

    A straight line on it is a constant compass bearing.

    WhyA navigator could draw a line from port to port, read one angle, and steer by it for the whole voyage.

  3. A map has a scale of 1 : 100,000. How long on the map is a 5 km road?

    Answer and why

    5 cm

    Why100,000 cm is 1 km, so each centimetre shows 1 km. Five kilometres need 5 cm.

  4. What is the latitude of the North Pole?

    Answer and why

    90° N

    WhyLatitude counts degrees from the equator (0°) to the pole, which is a quarter of a full circle: 90°.

  5. A city sits at 0° latitude, 78° W. What can you say about it at once?

    Answer and why

    It lies on the equator, in the western hemisphere.

    Why0° latitude is the equator itself. (This is close to Quito, in Ecuador, a country named after the equator.)

  6. Which projection would you choose to compare the size of Brazil with the size of Europe? Why?

    Answer and why

    An equal-area projection

    WhyIt keeps area true, so sizes can be compared fairly even though shapes are stretched.

★ Scholar's challenge

Aeroplanes from Mexico City to Tokyo fly north over Alaska instead of straight west across the Pacific. On a flat Mercator map this looks like a detour. Explain why it is actually the shortest route.

Answer and why

The shortest path on a sphere is a great circle, which looks curved on a Mercator map.

WhyStretch a string between the two cities on a globe and it bends north. The Mercator map distorts distance, so its "straight" lines are not the shortest ones.

Words to own

Cartography
The making and study of maps.
Projection
A method for showing the curved Earth on a flat surface.
Latitude
Distance north or south of the equator, in degrees.
Longitude
Distance east or west of the prime meridian, in degrees.
Scale
The ratio between a distance on a map and the real distance.
Great circle
The shortest route between two points on a sphere.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • If every map distorts something, can any map be called "true"?
  • Most world maps put north at the top and Europe near the centre. Is that a fact of nature or a choice? What would change if south were at the top?
  • Can a map change how people think about which countries are important?
