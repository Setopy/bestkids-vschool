⚛ Science · Week 7

Separation Techniques

The big questionSea water is a mixture of water and salt that no sieve in the world can separate. Yet a sailor stranded on a raft could make drinking water from it with a plastic sheet and the Sun. How?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Distinguish a pure substance from a mixture, and a solution from other mixtures.
  2. Choose a separation method according to the properties of the substances.
  3. Explain filtration, evaporation, distillation and chromatography with particles.
  4. Plan a multi-step separation.

1Mixtures and solutions

A pure substance contains only one kind of substance: one element or one compound. A mixture contains two or more substances that are not chemically bonded. Each keeps its own properties, so a mixture can be separated by physical means.

A solution is a mixture in which one substance has dissolved in another so evenly that you cannot see the parts.

TermMeaningIn salt water
SoluteThe substance that dissolvesSalt
SolventThe liquid it dissolves inWater
SolutionThe mixture formedSalt water
SolubleAble to dissolveSalt is soluble in water
InsolubleNot able to dissolveSand is insoluble in water

2Match the method to the property

Every separation uses a difference in a physical property. First ask: what is different about these substances?

3How the main methods work

Filtration. Filter paper has holes too small for solid grains but large enough for water particles and dissolved particles. The solid left on the paper is the residue. The liquid that passes through is the filtrate.

Evaporation. Heat the solution. The solvent particles escape as gas, and the solute is left behind as crystals. You keep the solid and lose the liquid.

Distillation. Heat the solution so the solvent boils, lead the vapour through a cooled tube where it condenses, and collect the pure liquid, the distillate. You keep the liquid. This is how fresh water is made from sea water.

Chromatography. Put a spot of ink near the bottom of a strip of absorbent paper and stand the end in water. As the water climbs it carries the dyes. Dyes that dissolve well travel far. Dyes that cling to the paper lag behind. The ink separates into bands of colour.

4Planning a separation

Real mixtures need several steps in the right order. Deal with one substance at a time, and ask at each step what property makes it different from the rest.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Plan the separation of a mixture of sand, salt and iron filings so that all three are recovered dry.

  1. Iron is magnetic and the others are not. Pass a magnet over the mixture to remove the iron filings.
  2. Salt is soluble and sand is not. Add warm water and stir until the salt dissolves.
  3. Filter. The sand is the residue. The salt solution is the filtrate.
  4. Dry the sand. Evaporate the water from the filtrate to leave salt crystals.

Answer: Magnet, then dissolve, then filter, then evaporate. Each step uses a different property.

Hands-on: Ink Detective and the Solar Still

You need: white coffee filters or kitchen paper, water-based felt-tip pens (include black), a glass, water, a large bowl, a small cup, cling film, a pebble, salt

  1. Chromatography. Cut a paper strip. Draw a pencil line 2 cm from one end and put a dot of black ink on it.
  2. Hang the strip in a glass with the end in 1 cm of water, the dot above the water. Watch for 10 minutes. How many colours are in "black"? Test three other pens.
  3. Solar still. Dissolve two spoonfuls of salt in a cup of water and pour it into the large bowl. Stand the empty small cup in the middle.
  4. Cover the bowl with cling film, and put the pebble on the film above the cup so it dips. Leave it in strong sunshine for several hours.
  5. Taste one drop of the water collected in the cup. Name each change of state that happened, and explain where the salt is now.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What is the difference between a mixture and a compound?

    Answer and why

    In a mixture the substances are not chemically bonded. In a compound they are.

    WhyA mixture's parts keep their own properties and can be separated physically. A compound needs a chemical reaction to split it.

  2. In sugary tea, name the solute and the solvent.

    Answer and why

    Sugar is the solute and water is the solvent.

    WhyThe solute is the substance that dissolves, and the solvent is the liquid that does the dissolving.

  3. Which method would you use to get sand out of water, and what is the sand called afterwards?

    Answer and why

    Filtration. The sand is the residue.

    WhySand grains are too large to pass through the holes in filter paper, while water passes through as the filtrate.

  4. Why can filtration not separate salt from salt water?

    Answer and why

    Dissolved salt particles are small enough to pass through the filter paper.

    WhyIn a solution the solute is broken down into individual particles, far smaller than the paper's holes.

  5. You want the water, not the salt, from sea water. Which method do you use?

    Answer and why

    Distillation

    WhyEvaporation would lose the water to the air. Distillation condenses and collects it.

  6. In chromatography, why do some dyes travel further up the paper than others?

    Answer and why

    They dissolve better in the water and cling less to the paper.

    WhyEach dye is pulled along by the solvent and held back by the paper. The balance is different for each.

★ Scholar's challenge

A sailor's bag has burst, mixing rice, salt, fine sand and steel pins. Using only fresh water, a cooking pot, a magnet, a cloth and a sieve whose holes are smaller than rice, write a plan to recover all four, and state the property used at each step.

Answer and why

Magnet (pins), sieve (rice), add water and filter through the cloth (sand), evaporate the filtrate (salt).

WhyPins are magnetic. Rice grains are larger than the sieve holes, while salt and sand fall through. Salt dissolves but sand does not, so the cloth holds back the sand. Boiling the water away leaves the salt.

Words to own

Mixture
Two or more substances together but not chemically bonded.
Solution
A mixture in which a solute is evenly dissolved in a solvent.
Filtrate
The liquid that passes through a filter.
Residue
The solid left behind on a filter.
Distillation
Separating by boiling a liquid and condensing its vapour.
Chromatography
Separating dissolved substances by how far they travel through a material.
