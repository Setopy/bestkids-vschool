French Immersion I: Greetings

The big questionFrench is an official language in 29 countries on five continents, and about a third of English words came from it. If you already know Spanish and English, how much French do you know without ever having studied it?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Greet, thank and take leave of someone politely in French.
  2. Introduce yourself and ask someone's name and how they are.
  3. Choose correctly between tu and vous.
  4. Apply four key rules of French pronunciation.

1A language you half know

French is a Romance language, a daughter of Latin and a sister of Spanish. Compare:

And English borrowed thousands of French words after the Norman Conquest of 1066: table, fruit, court, justice, beef, colour, question. Use what you know.

2Four pronunciation rules

  • Final consonants are usually silent. Salut is said "sa-LÜ". Vous is "voo". Petit is "puh-TEE".
  • oi is said "wa". Moi is "mwa". Au revoir is "oh ruh-VWAHR".
  • ou is "oo" as in "food". Vous is "voo". u alone is a sound English lacks: say "ee" with your lips rounded as for "oo".
  • Nasal vowels. When a vowel is followed by n or m at the end of a syllable, the n is not pronounced. The vowel is hummed through the nose: bon, un, bien, enchanté.

The French r is made at the back of the throat, like a soft gargle. The letter h is always silent. j is the "zh" sound /ʒ/, as in the middle of "measure".

5Tu or vous?

French has two words for "you", just as Spanish has tú and usted.

Use with
tuOne person you know well: family, friends, children, pets
vousAn adult you do not know well, a teacher, a shopkeeper; and always for more than one person

When in doubt, use vous. It is never rude to be too polite.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Read this dialogue aloud and translate it. "— Bonjour ! Comment tu t'appelles ? — Salut ! Je m'appelle Léa. Et toi ? — Je m'appelle Marc. Ça va ? — Ça va bien, merci. — Au revoir, Léa ! — À bientôt !"

  1. Bonjour / Salut are greetings. The speakers use tu, so they are children or friends.
  2. Je m'appelle gives the name. Et toi ? returns the question.
  3. Ça va ? asks how someone is, and Ça va bien, merci answers.
  4. Au revoir and À bientôt take leave.

Answer: "Hello! What is your name? — Hi! My name is Léa. And you? — My name is Marc. How are you? — I am well, thanks. — Goodbye, Léa! — See you soon!"

Hands-on: Le Café des Amis

You need: name badges, a table, two or more people

  1. Write a French name badge for everyone at home.
  2. Stand a "café" sign on the table. Each person enters, greets everyone, and introduces themselves in French.
  3. One person plays an adult stranger. Greet them using vous. Then greet a "friend" using tu.
  4. Record yourself reading the dialogue above. Listen for silent final letters and for the "wa" in au revoir and toi.
  5. For one whole day, say bonjour, merci, s'il vous plaît and bonne nuit at home in French only.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. How do you say "My name is Sofia" in French?

    Answer and why

    Je m'appelle Sofia.

    WhyLiterally "I call myself Sofia", built just like the Spanish "Me llamo Sofia".

  2. You meet your friend's grandmother for the first time. Do you say tu or vous?

    Answer and why

    Vous

    WhyShe is an adult you do not know well, so the polite form is correct.

  3. How is "oi" pronounced in moi, toi and au revoir?

    Answer and why

    "wa"

    WhyThe spelling oi always stands for the sound /wa/ in French.

  4. Translate: "Bonsoir ! Ça va ? — Comme ci, comme ça."

    Answer and why

    "Good evening! How are you? — So-so."

    WhyBonsoir is used in the evening, and comme ci, comme ça literally means "like this, like that".

  5. Why is the final t of salut not pronounced?

    Answer and why

    Final consonants in French are usually silent.

    WhyThe letters were once pronounced and remain in the spelling, like the k in English "knee".

  6. What is the French for "please", and what does it literally mean?

    Answer and why

    S'il vous plaît: "if it pleases you"

    Whysi (if) + il (it) + vous (you) + plaît (pleases). Compare English "if you please".

★ Scholar's challenge

Without a dictionary, work out the meaning of this sentence using Spanish, English and Latin roots: "Mon ami est très intelligent et il parle trois langues."

Answer and why

"My friend is very intelligent and he speaks three languages."

Whyami is like Spanish amigo; intelligent is the same in English; trois is the number root tri-; langues is like "language" and Spanish lenguas; parle resembles "parley" and "parliament", from parler, to speak.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • English lost its informal "thou" centuries ago. Did English lose something useful?
