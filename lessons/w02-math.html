Divisibility & Primes

The big questionEvery whole number greater than 1 can be built by multiplying prime numbers, and in only one way. What are these "atoms" of arithmetic, and how do you find them?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Use divisibility rules for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 and 10 without dividing.
  2. Decide whether a number is prime or composite.
  3. Write the prime factorisation of a number with a factor tree.
  4. Use prime factors to find the greatest common factor and least common multiple.

1Factors, multiples and divisibility

If 3 × 8 = 24, then 3 and 8 are factors of 24, and 24 is a multiple of 3 and of 8. We say 24 is divisible by 3 because the division leaves no remainder.

Divisibility rules let you test large numbers in seconds.

Why the 9 rule works: 10, 100 and 1,000 are each one more than a multiple of 9 (9 + 1, 99 + 1, 999 + 1). So each digit leaves behind exactly itself as a remainder, and only the digit sum matters. The 3 rule works for the same reason.

2Primes and composites

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. A composite number has more than two. The number 1 is neither, because it has only one factor.

The primes below 30 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 and 29. Two is the only even prime.

To test whether a number is prime, try dividing by primes in order. You may stop once the prime times itself is bigger than your number. To test 97, try 2, 3, 5 and 7. The next prime is 11, and 11 × 11 = 121 is more than 97, so you can stop. None divide 97, so it is prime.

3Prime factorisation

The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic says every whole number greater than 1 is either prime or a product of primes in exactly one way (apart from order). A factor tree finds that product: keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime.

We collect repeated primes with exponents: 2 × 2 × 2 = 2³.

4GCF and LCM from prime factors

  • The greatest common factor (GCF) uses each shared prime the fewest times it appears.
  • The least common multiple (LCM) uses every prime the most times it appears.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

Find the prime factorisation of 360.

  1. 360 = 36 × 10
  2. 36 = 6 × 6 = 2 × 3 × 2 × 3
  3. 10 = 2 × 5
  4. Collect: three 2s, two 3s, one 5.
  5. Check: 8 × 9 × 5 = 360. ✓

Answer: 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5

Example 2

Find the GCF and LCM of 84 and 126.

  1. 84 = 2² × 3 × 7
  2. 126 = 2 × 3² × 7
  3. GCF takes the fewest of each shared prime: 2 × 3 × 7 = 42.
  4. LCM takes the most of each prime: 2² × 3² × 7 = 252.
  5. Check: GCF × LCM = 42 × 252 = 10,584, and 84 × 126 = 10,584. ✓

Answer: GCF = 42, LCM = 252

Hands-on: The Sieve of Eratosthenes

You need: a 1–100 number grid, four coloured pencils

  1. Cross out 1. It is not prime.
  2. Circle 2, then cross out every other multiple of 2 in one colour.
  3. Circle 3, then cross out its remaining multiples in a second colour. Do the same for 5 and 7.
  4. Circle every number still standing. These are the 25 primes below 100.
  5. Explain why you did not need to sieve with 11. (Hint: 11 × 11.)

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. Without dividing, is 7,236 divisible by 4? By 9?

    Answer and why

    Yes to both.

    WhyThe last two digits, 36, are a multiple of 4. The digit sum 7+2+3+6 = 18 is a multiple of 9.

  2. Is 91 prime?

    Answer and why

    No. 91 = 7 × 13.

    WhyTesting primes in order: 2, 3 and 5 fail, but 7 divides 91 exactly 13 times. It is a famous "looks prime" number.

  3. Write the prime factorisation of 84.

    Answer and why

    2² × 3 × 7

    Why84 = 4 × 21 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7. Every factor is prime, so the tree is finished.

  4. Find the missing digit so that 5▢2 is divisible by 9.

    Answer and why

    2 (the number is 522)

    WhyThe known digits add to 7. The next multiple of 9 is 9, so the missing digit is 2. Check: 522 ÷ 9 = 58.

  5. Why is 1 not a prime number?

    Answer and why

    It has only one factor, and a prime needs exactly two.

    WhyIf 1 were prime, factorisations would no longer be unique: 6 could be 2 × 3 or 1 × 2 × 3 or 1 × 1 × 2 × 3.

  6. Two lighthouses flash every 12 seconds and every 18 seconds. They flash together now. When do they next flash together?

    Answer and why

    After 36 seconds

    WhyWe need the LCM. 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3², so LCM = 2² × 3² = 36.

  7. Is 4,356 divisible by 6?

    Answer and why

    Yes

    WhyIt is even (passes the 2 test) and its digit sum 18 is a multiple of 3. A number divisible by both 2 and 3 is divisible by 6.

★ Scholar's challenge

What is the smallest number divisible by every whole number from 1 to 10?

Answer and why

2,520

WhyTake each prime the most times it is needed: 2³ (for 8), 3² (for 9), 5 and 7. Then 8 × 9 × 5 × 7 = 2,520.

Words to own

Factor
A whole number that divides another exactly.
Multiple

Prime
A number with exactly two factors, 1 and itself.
Composite
A number with more than two factors.
Exponent
