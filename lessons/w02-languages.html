Ω Languages · Week 2

Phonetics

The big questionEnglish has 26 letters but about 44 sounds. The letters "ough" are said differently in though, through, cough and bough. If spelling cannot be trusted, how can we write down exactly what a word sounds like?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to

  1. Explain the difference between a letter and a sound.
  2. Describe consonants by voicing, place and manner.
  3. Read simple words written in the International Phonetic Alphabet.
  4. Count the sounds in a word, ignoring its spelling.

1Letters are not sounds

Phonetics is the study of speech sounds. A single speech sound is called a phone, and a sound that can change the meaning of a word in a language is a phoneme. Swap one phoneme and ship becomes sheep, pat becomes bat.

Spelling is an old and imperfect record. One sound can have many spellings (the "f" sound in fish, phone, laugh), and one spelling can have many sounds ("ough"). Spanish spelling is far more regular: it has five clear vowel sounds, a e i o u, while English has about twenty vowel sounds.

2The International Phonetic Alphabet

In 1888 language teachers created the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA). Its rule is simple: one symbol, one sound, for every language on Earth. IPA is written between slashes.

3How consonants are made

Every consonant can be described by three facts.

  • Voicing. Put your fingers on your throat. Say "zzzz", then "ssss". If your throat buzzes, the sound is voiced. If not, it is voiceless.
  • Place. Where the air is blocked: both lips (p, b, m), teeth and lip (f, v), tongue behind the teeth (t, d, n, s, z), back of the mouth (k, g).
  • Manner. How the air is blocked: stopped completely and released (stops: p, t, k), squeezed with friction (fricatives: f, s, sh), or sent through the nose (nasals: m, n, ng).

4Vowels

Vowels are made with an open mouth and no blocking. They differ by how high the tongue is, whether it is forward or back, and whether the lips are rounded. Say "ee … oo" slowly and feel the tongue slide back and the lips round.

Worked examples

Cover the steps. Predict each line before you read it.

Example 1

How many sounds are in the word "through"?

  1. Ignore the seven letters. Say the word slowly.
  2. "th" is one sound: /θ/.
  3. "r" is one sound: /r/.
  4. "ough" here is one long vowel: /uː/.

Answer: Three sounds: /θruː/

Example 2

How many sounds are in "box"?

  1. b is /b/. o is one vowel.
  2. The letter x hides two sounds: /k/ and /s/.

Answer: Four sounds: /bɒks/. One letter can stand for two sounds.

Hands-on: The Mirror and the Throat

You need: a small mirror, a strip of thin paper, a notebook

  1. Hold the paper strip in front of your lips. Say "pin", then "bin". Which makes the paper jump more? That puff of air is called aspiration.
  2. Touch your throat and sort these sounds into voiced and voiceless: s, z, f, v, t, d, sh, m.
  3. In the mirror, watch your lips for "ee", "ah", "oo". Draw the three lip shapes.
  4. Find five minimal pairs: word pairs that differ by a single sound, such as ship and sheep, or fan and van.
  5. Write your own name in IPA as well as you can, using the table.

Practice

Write your full working in your notebook before opening an answer.

  1. What is the difference between a letter and a phoneme?

    Answer and why

    A letter is a written symbol. A phoneme is a meaningful unit of sound.

    WhyWriting and speech are separate systems. English spelling often uses several letters for one sound, or one letter for several.

  2. How many sounds are in "knight"?

    Answer and why

    Three: /naɪt/

    WhyThe k and gh are silent. The sounds are n, the vowel "eye", and t.

  3. Is /z/ voiced or voiceless? How can you check?

    Answer and why

    Voiced. Your throat vibrates when you say it.

    WhyThe vocal cords buzz for /z/ and stay still for its partner /s/.

  4. Which sound does the IPA symbol /ʃ/ stand for? Give two words spelled differently that contain it.

    Answer and why

    The "sh" sound, as in ship, nation, sugar or machine.

    WhyIPA has one symbol per sound, however the sound happens to be spelled.

  5. What is the only difference between the sounds /p/ and /b/?

    Answer and why

    Voicing: /p/ is voiceless and /b/ is voiced.

    WhyBoth are stops made with both lips. The vocal cords vibrate only for /b/.

  6. How many sounds are in "cheese"?

    Answer and why

    Three: /tʃiːz/

    Why"ch" is one sound, "ee" is one vowel, and the final "se" is a single /z/. The last e is silent.

★ Scholar's challenge

Spanish speakers learning English often say "sheep" and "ship" the same way, and English speakers often cannot roll the Spanish "rr". Explain both facts with what you know about phonemes.

Answer and why

Each language has its own set of phonemes, and learners hear new sounds through the set they already own.

WhySpanish has one "i" vowel where English has two, /iː/ and /ɪ/, so the difference is hard to hear at first. English has no trilled r, so the tongue has never practised it.

Socratic table talk

Discuss aloud. There is no answer key: defend your thinking with reasons.

  • Should English spelling be reformed so that every sound has exactly one spelling? What would be gained and what lost?
  • Babies can hear every speech sound of every language. Why might adults lose that ability?
  • Is an accent a mistake? Who decides which pronunciation is "correct"?
