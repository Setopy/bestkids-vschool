Week 4

Five lessons. One per subject. Work in order or follow your timetable.

∑ Mathematics

Bar Modeling I: Part-Whole and Comparison

Pupils in Singapore are among the best problem-solvers in the world, and one of their main tools is a simple rectangle. How can drawing a bar make a hard word problem easy?

Open lesson → ⚛ Science

The Periodic Table

In 1869 a Russian chemist left empty spaces in his chart of the elements and described three elements nobody had ever seen. Within twenty years all three were found, almost exactly as he predicted. How can a table predict the unknown?

Open lesson → 🏛 History & Geography

Hydrology

Earth is called the blue planet, yet if all its water filled a 100-litre barrel, the fresh water people can easily reach would be less than a teaspoon. Where is all the water, and why did every early civilisation grow beside a river?

Open lesson → ✒ Literature

Norse Myth

The Greek gods live for ever. The Norse gods know the exact day they will die and how, and they march out to fight anyway. What kind of people tell stories in which even the gods lose?

Open lesson → Ω Languages

Latin & Greek Number Roots I

A unicycle has one wheel, a bicycle two and a tricycle three. A monologue has one speaker, and a pentagon has five sides. Why does English have two complete sets of counting words hidden inside it?
